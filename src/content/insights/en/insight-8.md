Although an LLC is a U.S. legal entity, each state has its own rules regarding:

- Maintenance costs.
- Annual reports.
- Member privacy.
- State obligations.
- Administrative ease.
- Business reputation.
- Registered agent requirements.

That is why forming an LLC without analyzing the correct state can lead to unnecessary costs or compliance issues.

## LLC in Wyoming

Wyoming is often an attractive option for international entrepreneurs due to its low costs, privacy, and ease of maintenance.

Wyoming's annual report is calculated with a minimum fee of USD 60 or 0.0002 on assets located and used in Wyoming, whichever is greater. Additionally, the annual report is due on the first day of the entity's formation anniversary month.

### Advantages of Wyoming

- Generally low annual costs.
- Good member privacy.
- Simple maintenance.
- Popular for digital businesses, consultants, and freelancers.
- Good option for companies with no physical U.S. operations.

### Who is Wyoming good for?

Wyoming can be a good alternative for:

- Freelancers.
- Consultants.
- Digital agencies.
- Online businesses.
- Service exporters.
- Latin American entrepreneurs without a physical U.S. office.

## LLC in Florida

Florida is a very common option for Latin American entrepreneurs due to its commercial proximity, language, banking presence, and perception of trust.

Florida LLCs must file their annual report between January 1 and May 1. If not filed on time, the state imposes a late penalty of USD 400.

### Advantages of Florida

- Well known among Latin American entrepreneurs.
- Good commercial image in LATAM.
- Access to banks, offices, and commercial partners.
- Useful if the business will have physical presence or a strong relationship with Florida.
- Ideal for real estate, commerce, professional services, or businesses with local operations.

### Who is Florida good for?

Florida may be recommended for:

- Entrepreneurs with physical presence in Florida.
- Real estate investors.
- Businesses with an office, employees, or suppliers in Florida.
- Companies wanting to project commercial proximity with the Latin American market in the U.S.
- Clients planning to open an in-person bank account or have a more visible operation in the state.

## LLC in Delaware

Delaware is one of the most recognized states for corporate structures in the United States, especially for its legal tradition and reputation in the business world.

For LLCs, Delaware does not require filing an annual report, but it does require payment of a fixed annual tax of USD 300 before June 1.

### Advantages of Delaware

- High corporate reputation.
- Widely used by startups, investors, and holding structures.
- Recognized legal framework.
- No annual report required for LLCs.
- Attractive for companies with investment plans, expansion, or more sophisticated structures.

### Who is Delaware good for?

Delaware can be a good option for:

- Startups.
- Companies seeking investment.
- Holdings.
- More complex corporate structures.
- Businesses with international partners.
- Companies wanting a corporate jurisdiction with solid reputation.

## So, which one should you choose?

It depends on your case.

### Choose Wyoming if:

You want a simple, low-maintenance structure with good privacy and no physical U.S. operations.

### Choose Florida if:

You have physical presence, clients, suppliers, banks, real estate investments, or a business strategy tied to Florida.

### Choose Delaware if:

You are creating a startup, holding, investment structure, or a company that needs a highly recognized corporate jurisdiction.

## Common mistake: choosing a state just because it's trendy

Many entrepreneurs form their LLC in a state because they saw it on social media, without analyzing:

- Where the business will actually operate.
- Whether it will have employees or inventory.
- Whether it will sell within the U.S.
- Whether it will need to register as a foreign LLC in another state.
- What tax obligations it will have.
- What annual costs it will need to pay.

This can cause additional expenses and compliance issues.

## Conclusion

Wyoming, Florida, and Delaware can all be excellent options, but each one responds to a different need.

The best decision depends not only on cost but on the complete business strategy.

A well-structured LLC can help you operate internationally, protect your assets, and access the U.S. financial system. But a poorly chosen LLC can generate unnecessary costs and future risks.
