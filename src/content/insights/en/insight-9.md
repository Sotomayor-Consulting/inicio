Many people focus only on the cost of forming an LLC, but the most important question is: **how much does it cost to maintain one?**

Maintaining an LLC typically involves several components:

- Registered agent.
- State annual report.
- Franchise or state fees.
- Informational filings with the IRS.
- Accounting or bookkeeping.
- Corporate renewals.
- Licenses or permits (if applicable).

The total cost will depend on:

- The state where the LLC was formed.
- The business activity.
- Whether it has income or not.
- Whether it has multiple members.
- Whether it generates additional tax obligations.

## Registered Agent

Every LLC must have a registered agent in the state where it was incorporated.

The registered agent is the person or company authorized to receive legal documents and official state notifications.

### Approximate cost

Typically ranges from **USD 50 to USD 300 per year**. The value depends on the provider and the state.

## State Annual Report

Most states require the LLC to file an annual report to remain active. This report serves to update corporate information and confirm that the company is still operating.

### Examples by state

| State | Annual Cost | Notes |
|-------|------------|-------|
| **Wyoming** | $60 minimum | Fee based on assets or $60 minimum |
| **Florida** | $138.75 | $400 late penalty if not filed on time |
| **Delaware** | $300 | No Annual Report required but fixed Franchise Tax |

## Franchise Tax or State Fees

Some states charge annual taxes or fees simply for keeping the company active. This does not necessarily mean the LLC is paying income tax. Often it is simply a state maintenance cost.

### Important

Each state has different rules. For example:

- Delaware has a fixed Franchise Tax for LLCs.
- Wyoming has low fees.
- California has significantly higher costs ($800 minimum).
- Florida has a mandatory Annual Report.

## Tax or Informational Filings

This is where many people get confused. Even if an LLC has no income, it may have obligations before the IRS.

### Common examples

**Single-member foreign LLC:** In many cases must file Form 5472 and informational Form 1120.

**Multi-member LLC:** May require Form 1065 and K-1 for members.

**LLC with U.S. activity:** Depending on the case, there may be federal tax obligations, state obligations, and additional filings.

### How much does this cost?

It depends on complexity. Costs can range from basic informational services to much more complex accounting and tax structures.

## Accounting and Bookkeeping

Many LLCs need to track income, expenses, financial statements, transaction classification, and bank reconciliations. Especially if:

- They operate with Stripe.
- They use U.S. banks.
- They sell online.
- They handle multiple transactions.

### Common mistake

Many entrepreneurs mix personal and business expenses, which can lead to tax problems, accounting issues, banking risks, and loss of financial order.

## Licenses and Permits

Depending on the type of activity, the LLC may need:

- Business licenses.
- Seller's Permit.
- Local permits.
- Special registrations.

This depends heavily on the business model and the state.

## Real Cost Scenarios

### Basic scenario

A simple LLC with no complex operations:

| Item | Annual cost |
|------|-------------|
| Registered agent | $100 |
| State report | $60–$139 |
| Informational filings | $200–$500 |
| **Approximate total** | **$360–$739** |

### Intermediate scenario

If the company has bank activity, Stripe, e-commerce, or members:

| Item | Annual cost |
|------|-------------|
| Registered agent | $100–$200 |
| State report | $60–$300 |
| Tax filings | $500–$1500 |
| Monthly bookkeeping | $600–$1800 |
| **Approximate total** | **$1260–$3800** |

### Advanced scenario

Companies with employees, physical operations, inventory, or holding structures:

| Item | Annual cost |
|------|-------------|
| Registered agent | $200–$300 |
| State report + taxes | $300–$800+ |
| Tax filings | $1500–$5000+ |
| Monthly bookkeeping | $1800–$6000+ |
| Licenses and permits | $100–$1000+ |
| **Approximate total** | **$3900–$13100+** |

## What happens if I don't maintain my LLC?

Failing to meet obligations can result in:

- Fines.
- Loss of the LLC.
- State suspension.
- Problems with banks.
- Problems with payment processors.
- Tax risks.
- Future difficulties reactivating the company.

In some cases, even an "unused" LLC may still have informational filing obligations.

## Very common mistake in Latin America

Many people open LLCs thinking "there's nothing to declare", "nothing happens if I don't use it", or "I only pay once."

**This is incorrect.** Opening the LLC is just the beginning. Maintaining it correctly is equally important.

## Is maintaining an LLC worth it?

If properly structured and aligned with your business, an LLC can provide tremendous value:

- International financial access.
- Professionalization.
- Asset protection.
- Global expansion.
- Dollar operations.
- International credibility.

But for it to really work, it must remain in compliance.

## Conclusion

The cost of maintaining an LLC depends entirely on the state chosen, the type of activity, the actual business operations, and the applicable tax obligations.

That is why, before forming an LLC, it is important to understand not only how much it costs to incorporate it, but how much it will cost to maintain it correctly each year.
