If you have neglected your LLC's obligations �whether the Annual Report, federal taxes, or corporate documentation� you are not alone. Many international entrepreneurs find themselves in this situation due to lack of knowledge, changes of residence, or simply lack of time.

The good news is that **it is almost always possible to regularize your LLC**, although the process may be more or less complex depending on how long it has been and the state of your company.

In this guide, we explain step by step how to regularize your LLC in every possible situation.

## What situation are you in?

### Situation 1: Overdue Annual Report (LLC suspended)

This is the most common situation. If you did not file the Annual Report in your state, the LLC goes into "suspended" or "not in good standing" status.

**Immediate consequences:**
- You lose limited liability protection.
- You cannot open bank accounts in the LLC's name.
- The bank (Mercury, Relay) may freeze your account.
- You cannot renew contracts or registrations.
- The state may dissolve the LLC after several years.

#### Step by step to regularize

| Step | Action | Detail |
|------|--------|---------|
| 1 | Identify the state | Check the Secretary of State where you formed the LLC |
| 2 | Verify status | Look up your LLC on the Secretary of State portal |
| 3 | Calculate what you owe | Late Annual Report + penalties + interest |
| 4 | File the reports | File all missing Annual Reports |
| 5 | Pay penalties | Generally paid online |
| 6 | Request reinstatement | If the LLC was dissolved, you need an additional process |
| 7 | Confirm status | Verify the LLC shows as "Active" or "Good Standing" |

#### Typical costs by state

| State | Annual Report | Penalty per late year | Reinstatement |
|--------|--------------|----------------------|---------------|
| **Wyoming** | $60 | $60 + $1/day | $60 + penalties |
| **Florida** | $138.75 | $400 | $400 + reports |
| **Delaware** | $300 (franchise tax) | $200 + 1.5%/month | $200 + penalties |
| **New Mexico** | Not required | N/A | N/A |

> **Important:** In Wyoming, if you do not file the Annual Report for more than 2-3 years, the LLC may be administratively revoked. In Florida, the penalty for not filing is $400 plus late reports.

### Situation 2: Form 5472 not filed (IRS)

If you are a foreign owner of a single-member LLC and have not filed Form 5472, you are exposed to penalties of **$25,000 per year**.

#### Step by step to regularize

| Step | Action | Detail |
|------|--------|---------|
| 1 | Gather information | EIN, transactions for each year, personal data |
| 2 | Prepare late forms | 5472 + pro-forma 1120 for each year |
| 3 | Consider the Delinquent Filing program | The IRS may reduce penalties if you file voluntarily |
| 4 | File everything together | Do not file only the 5472; it must go with the 1120 |
| 5 | Evaluate if you need a CPA | Recommended for late years |
| 6 | Respond to any IRS letter | If the IRS already contacted you, respond immediately |

**IRS delinquent filing program:**

The IRS has a special program for taxpayers who have not filed required forms. If you file voluntarily before the IRS notifies you:

- Penalties can be significantly reduced.
- The IRS may waive penalties if you show reasonable cause.
- You must file **all** late years.

> **Warning:** If the IRS has already sent you a letter or notice, the delinquent filing program no longer applies. In that case, you must respond to the letter and possibly pay penalties.

### Situation 3: Form 1065 not filed (multi-member LLC)

If your LLC has two or more members and you have not filed Form 1065, penalties are $220 per month per partner.

#### Step by step to regularize

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Gather financial statements for all late years |
| 2 | Determine each partner's share for each year |
| 3 | Prepare Form 1065 and K-1 for each late year |
| 4 | File all late years |
| 5 | Calculate accumulated penalties ($220/month/partner) |
| 6 | Evaluate if you can request penalty reduction |
| 7 | Deliver late K-1s to partners |

### Situation 4: Bank account frozen by Mercury or Relay

If your account was frozen due to lack of documentation or because the LLC is not in good standing, the regularization process is urgent.

#### Step by step

1. **Identify the cause of the freeze**
   - Did they request updated documents?
   - Did they detect the LLC is not in good standing?
   - Were there suspicious transactions?

2. **Regularize the LLC first**
   - If the problem is the Annual Report, file it and pay penalties.
   - Confirm the LLC is in "Good Standing".

3. **Prepare the requested documentation**
   - Updated Certificate of Formation.
   - EIN Letter.
   - Operating Agreement.
   - Updated proof of address.
   - Most recent Annual Report.

4. **Respond to the bank professionally**
   - Explain the situation.
   - Attach all documents.
   - Request the account release.

5. **Follow up**
   - If no response in 7 days, send a reminder.
   - If urgent, call or use the support chat.

### Situation 5: Dissolved or revoked LLC

If your LLC was dissolved by the state for years of non-compliance, you can still reinstate it in most cases.

#### Step by step

1. **Verify status with the Secretary of State.**
2. **Determine if administrative reinstatement applies** (simple process) or judicial (more complex).
3. **File all late Annual Reports.**
4. **Pay penalties and reinstatement fees.**
5. **Request a Certificate of Good Standing** once reinstated.
6. **Update your information with the IRS** (if anything changed).
7. **Notify your bank** that the LLC is regularized.

#### Time limits by state

| State | Maximum time to reinstate |
|--------|------------------------------|
| Wyoming | 5 years after dissolution |
| Florida | 5 years after dissolution |
| Delaware | 3 years after dissolution |
| New Mexico | Not applicable (no Annual Report required) |

> If you exceed the time limit, you may need to form a new LLC.

## General regularization strategy

### Step 1: Diagnosis

Determine exactly which obligations are overdue:

- [ ] State Annual Report (how many years?).
- [ ] Form 5472 (how many years?).
- [ ] Form 1065 (how many years?).
- [ ] BOI Form (Corporate Transparency Act).
- [ ] State tax returns (if applicable).
- [ ] Expired Registered Agent.

### Step 2: Prioritize

| Priority | Obligation | Reason |
|-----------|-----------|--------|
| **High** | Annual Report | Your LLC can be dissolved |
| **High** | Frozen bank account | Your business cannot operate |
| **High** | BOI | $10,000 penalty |
| **Medium** | Form 5472 | $25,000/year penalty |
| **Medium** | Form 1065 | $220/month/partner penalty |
| **Low** | Expired Registered Agent | Low cost, easy to resolve |

### Step 3: Execute

Resolve each obligation in order of priority. Some can be resolved in parallel.

### Step 4: Verify

After regularizing, confirm:

- [ ] The LLC is in "Good Standing" in the state.
- [ ] IRS forms were processed.
- [ ] The bank released the account (if frozen).
- [ ] The BOI was filed.
- [ ] The Registered Agent is active.

### Step 5: Prevent

Once regularized, set up a system to avoid falling behind again:

- Compliance calendar with all dates.
- Reminders 30 days before each deadline.
- A professional (CPA or advisor) who notifies you.
- Reserve fund to pay Annual Reports and Registered Agent.

## Practical cases

### Case 1: Late Annual Report in Wyoming

**Situation:** Carla formed her LLC in Wyoming in 2021. She filed the Annual Report in 2022 but forgot 2023 and 2024. In January 2025, Mercury froze her account.

**What she did:**
1. Checked status in Wyoming: "Not in Good Standing".
2. Calculated what she owed: 2 Annual Reports ($60 each) = $120 + $60 penalty.
3. Filed both Annual Reports online.
4. Paid $180.
5. Waited 24 hours for the state to update the status.
6. Requested a Certificate of Good Standing.
7. Sent the certificate to Mercury.
8. Mercury released the account in 3 days.

**Total cost:** $180 + 1 week downtime.

### Case 2: Form 5472 not filed for 3 years

**Situation:** Peter has owned an LLC in Florida since 2020. He never filed Form 5472 because his local accountant said it "wasn't necessary." In 2024, the IRS sent him a compliance letter.

**What he did:**
1. Consulted with a CPA specialized in international LLCs.
2. Prepared Form 5472 + pro-forma 1120 for 2020, 2021, 2022, and 2023.
3. Filed everything together under the delinquent filing program.
4. Responded to the IRS letter explaining the situation.
5. The IRS did not impose penalties because he filed voluntarily before formal notification.

**Total cost:** $2,000 (CPA) + $0 in penalties (for voluntary filing).

### Case 3: Dissolved LLC in Florida

**Situation:** Andrew formed his LLC in Florida in 2018. He did not file Annual Reports since 2020. In 2024, the state dissolved the LLC. Andrew found out when Stripe suspended his account.

**What he did:**
1. Checked status: "Dissolved".
2. Consulted with a registered agent in Florida.
3. Filed Annual Reports for 2021, 2022, 2023, and 2024.
4. Paid penalties: 4 years � $400 = $1,600.
5. Requested administrative reinstatement.
6. Paid reinstatement fee.
7. Waited 2 weeks for the state to process.
8. Requested Certificate of Good Standing.
9. Sent it to Stripe.
10. Stripe reactivated the account.

**Total cost:** $1,600 in penalties + $400 in fees = $2,000 + 3 weeks downtime.

## When is it better to form a new LLC?

In some cases, regularizing an LLC can be more expensive than forming a new one.

### When to form a new LLC

- The LLC was dissolved more than 5 years ago and cannot be reinstated.
- Regularization costs exceed the cost of a new LLC.
- The LLC has legal problems or debts.
- You changed your state of residence and it is better to form in another state.
- The LLC was closed by the bank and you cannot open another account with the same EIN.

### When to regularize the current LLC

- The LLC has a tax history with the IRS.
- The LLC has active contracts in its name.
- The LLC has an active bank account (even if frozen).
- The LLC has recurring clients who already know the name.
- Regularization costs are lower than a new LLC.

## Prevention: How not to fall behind again

### Compliance system

| Tool | Purpose |
|-------------|-----------|
| **Shared calendar** | Annual Report, 5472, 1065, BOI due dates |
| **30-60-90 day reminders** | Alert 90, 60, and 30 days in advance |
| **Compliance fund** | Separate account to pay Annual Report and Registered Agent |
| **CPA or advisor** | Professional who reviews your compliance annually |
| **Reliable Registered Agent** | Notifies you of any communication |
| **Annual checklist** | Review all obligations at the start of each year |

## Conclusion

Regularizing an overdue LLC is possible in most cases, but the longer it goes, the more expensive and complex it becomes.

**Do not wait for the problem to get worse.** If you owe Annual Reports, have not filed Form 5472, or your bank account is frozen, act now.

Most regularizations are resolved in weeks, not months. And the cost of regularizing is almost always less than the cost of accumulated penalties or business loss.
