When forming an LLC in the United States, one of the most important —and least understood— aspects is how it is taxed. By default, the IRS classifies LLCs in one of two ways: **disregarded entity** or **partnership**.

The difference between them directly affects how much you pay in taxes, how you report your income, and what tax obligations you have. In this article, we explain everything you need to know to choose the right option.

## What does each regime mean?

### Disregarded Entity

Applies automatically to **single-member LLCs**. The IRS "ignores" the LLC as a separate entity for tax purposes.

**How it works:**

- The LLC does not file a separate tax return.
- The owner reports LLC income and expenses on their personal tax return.
- For foreign owners (non-residents), Form 5472 is filed as an information return.

**For foreign owners:** The IRS does not require you to file a U.S. income tax return if the LLC has no effectively connected income (ECI) in the U.S. However, you must file Form 5472 annually.

### Partnership

Applies automatically to **multi-member LLCs** (two or more members).

**How it works:**

- The LLC files an information return (Form 1065).
- Each member receives a Schedule K-1 reporting their share of profits or losses.
- Each member reports their share on their personal tax return.

**For foreign owners:** If you are a member of a multi-member LLC, the LLC must file Form 1065 and each foreign member must file their personal return (possibly Form 1040-NR).

## Quick comparison

- **Number of members:** 1 -- 2+
- **LLC filing:** N/A -- Form 1065
- **Owner reporting:** On personal return -- Schedule K-1
- **Form 5472:** Yes (foreign owner) -- Depends
- **Tax complexity:** Low -- Medium
- **Compliance cost:** Low -- Medium
- **Liability protection:** Same -- Same
- **Distribution flexibility:** N/A -- High
- **Ideal for:** Freelancers, sole owners -- Partners, agencies
## Which one to choose?

### Choose Disregarded Entity if:

1. **You are the sole owner** of the LLC.
2. **You have moderate income** and want tax simplicity.
3. **You are a foreigner** and want to minimize U.S. tax obligations.
4. **You do not plan to have investors** or additional members.
5. **You want lower** accounting and compliance costs.

### Choose Partnership if:

1. **You have two or more members** in the business.
2. **You want flexible profit distribution** (not necessarily equal).
3. **You plan to grow** and eventually bring in investors.
4. **You accept higher tax complexity** in exchange for flexibility.
5. **You need losses** that members can deduct on their personal returns.

## Deep dive: Disregarded Entity for foreign owners

This is the most common regime for Latin American entrepreneurs forming a U.S. LLC. It is important to understand its implications.

### Advantages

- **Simplicity:** No LLC filing, only Form 5472
- **Low cost:** No need for expensive CPA for filing
- **No double taxation:** Income taxed only in your country of residence
- **Privacy:** Less information reported to the IRS
- **Easy administration:** No K-1, no complex accounting
### Disadvantages

- **No tax separation:** IRS sees LLC and owner as the same entity
- **No deductible losses:** Cannot have losses that benefit the company
- **Limited to one owner:** Cannot have members without changing regime
### Tax obligations

If you are a foreigner with a single-member LLC:

1. **Form 5472:** Must be filed annually before April 15 (extension to October 15). The penalty for not filing is **$25,000** per year.
2. **Pro-forma 1120:** Although not mandatory, many accountants recommend filing a pro-forma 1120 to establish a solid tax basis.
3. **FBAR (FinCEN Form 114):** If the sum of your foreign financial accounts (including the LLC's bank account) exceeds $10,000 at any time during the year, you must file FBAR.
4. **FATCA (Form 8938):** If your specified foreign financial assets exceed certain thresholds (generally $50,000 for singles), you must file this form.

### Is Disregarded Entity the best option?

For most freelancers and sole owners, **yes**. It is simple, affordable, and effective. However, if you plan to scale quickly, have investors, or generate significant initial losses, you might consider other regimes.

## Deep dive: Partnership for multi-member LLCs

If you have members, the IRS requires you to be taxed as a partnership (unless you elect to be treated as a corporation, which is a separate topic).

### Advantages

- **Flexible distribution:** Can distribute profits in different proportions
- **Deductible losses:** Members can deduct losses on their personal returns
- **Capital contributions:** Easy structuring of member contributions
- **Scalability:** Easy to add or remove members
- **Transparency:** Each member knows their exact share
### Disadvantages

- **Higher tax complexity:** Form 1065, K-1, more detailed accounting
- **Higher cost:** Need a CPA to prepare the return
- **Obligations for foreign owners:** Each foreign member must file a return
- **Strict deadlines:** 1065 due March 15 (no extension)
- **Possible double taxation:** If not structured well, may pay taxes in U.S. and your country
### Considerations for foreign members

If you have a multi-member LLC with foreign members:

1. **The LLC must file Form 1065** annually.
2. **Each foreign member receives a K-1** and must report their share.
3. **Foreign members may need to file Form 1040-NR** if they have effectively connected income (ECI).
4. **Tax withholding:** The LLC may need to withhold taxes for foreign members in certain cases.

## Can I change regimes?

Yes, but with limitations.

### From Disregarded Entity to Partnership

Occurs automatically when you add a second member to your LLC.

**What you need to do:**

1. Update the Operating Agreement.
2. Obtain a new EIN (the IRS requires a new one for multi-member LLCs).
3. Notify your bank about the structure change (possible additional documentation).
4. From the change, file Form 1065.

### From Partnership to Disregarded Entity

Occurs when one member buys the other's share and only one remains.

**What you need to do:**

1. Update the Operating Agreement.
2. Obtain a new EIN.
3. Notify the bank.
4. From the change, file only Form 5472 (if you are foreign).

### From either to Corporation (S-Corp or C-Corp)

An LLC can elect to be treated as a corporation by filing Form 8832 (for C-Corp) or Form 2553 (for S-Corp). This is a strategic decision that should be evaluated with an accountant.

## Decision table

- **Individual freelancer, income < $100k/year:** Disregarded Entity -- Simplicity, low cost
- **Individual freelancer, income > $100k/year:** Disregarded Entity (evaluate S-Corp) -- Potential tax savings
- **Two or more members, equal percentages:** Partnership -- Required by IRS
- **Two or more members, different percentages:** Partnership -- Distribution flexibility
- **Foreign member + U.S. member:** Partnership (with advice) -- Cross-border compliance
- **Sole owner, plans to have investors:** Disregarded Entity (initial), then Partnership -- Scalability
- **Agency with multiple employees:** Partnership or S-Corp -- Protection and efficiency
## Practical case: Freelancer vs Agency with partners

### Case 1: Carlos, individual freelancer

Carlos is a web designer in Peru. He has his LLC in Wyoming and works alone.

- **Regime:** Disregarded Entity
- **EIN:** 1 (single-member)
- **Annual filing:** Form 5472
- **Annual tax cost:** ~$200 (5472 preparation)
- **Complexity:** Low
**Result:** Carlos pays ~$200/year in tax compliance, files his 5472, and LLC income is taxed only in Peru.

### Case 2: Maria and Juan's agency

Maria and Juan are 50% partners in a digital marketing agency with an LLC in Florida.

- **Regime:** Partnership
- **EIN:** 1 (multi-member)
- **Annual filing:** Form 1065 + K-1 for each member
- **Annual tax cost:** ~$1,500 (1065 + K-1s preparation)
- **Complexity:** Medium
**Result:** Maria and Juan pay ~$1,500/year, have more detailed accounting, but can distribute profits flexibly and deduct losses if any.

## Conclusion

The choice between disregarded entity and partnership depends on your business structure:

- **Single-member LLC = Disregarded Entity** by default. Simple, affordable, ideal for freelancers and sole owners.
- **Multi-member LLC = Partnership** by default. Flexible, scalable, ideal for agencies with partners.

Both regimes offer the same limited liability protection. The difference lies in tax complexity and flexibility.

If you are a sole owner, stick with disregarded entity. If you have partners or plan to, accept the additional complexity of a partnership and find a good CPA.
