One of the most confusing concepts for international entrepreneurs with LLCs is the difference between **tax evasion** (illegal) and **tax planning** (legal). The line may seem thin, but the consequences of crossing it are radically different.

In this guide, we explain what distinguishes one from the other, with concrete examples so you always stay on the right side of the law.

## What is tax planning?

It is the process of organizing your finances and business structure to **pay the minimum taxes required by law**, taking advantage of deductions, tax credits, international treaties, and legal structures.

### Characteristics

- **Transparency:** All income is declared
- **Legality:** Mechanisms provided by law are used
- **Documentation:** Everything is backed by invoices, contracts, and records
- **Sustainability:** The strategy is maintained over time
- **Professional:** Done with advice from accountants and lawyers
### Legitimate examples of tax planning

**Choosing the right state for your LLC.**
   - Choosing Wyoming over Delaware to save $240/year in franchise tax.
   - Legal basis: Freedom to choose where to form.

**Using the right tax structure.**
   - Single-member LLC as disregarded entity to avoid double taxation.
   - Legal basis: IRS allows this classification.

**Taking advantage of business deductions.**
   - Deducting software, equipment, internet, and necessary services.
   - Legal basis: Section 162 of the Internal Revenue Code.

**Claiming tax credits.**
   - Foreign Tax Credit for taxes paid in the U.S.
   - Legal basis: Internal Revenue Code Section 901.

**Using international tax treaties.**
   - Reducing withholding on interest and royalties.
   - Legal basis: Tax treaty between your country and the U.S.

**Separating business lines into different LLCs.**
   - Protecting assets of one line from liabilities of another.
   - Legal basis: U.S. corporate law.

## What is tax evasion?

It is the illegal action of **not paying the taxes that the law requires**, by hiding income, overstating expenses, using fake invoices, or transferring assets to avoid reporting.

### Characteristics

- **Concealment:** Undeclared income
- **Illegality:** Direct violation of the law
- **Lack of documentation:** No real backing for transactions
- **High risk:** Penalties, jail, business closure
- **Unsustainable:** Sooner or later the tax authority detects it
### Examples of tax evasion

**Not declaring LLC income.**
   - Receiving payments in a personal account and not reporting them to the IRS or local authority.
   - Consequence: Tax fraud, penalties up to 75% of unpaid taxes.

**Using fake invoices to inflate expenses.**
   - Buying invoices from nonexistent suppliers to reduce taxable profit.
   - Consequence: Criminal offense, possible jail time.

**Transferring assets to relatives to avoid taxes.**
   - Putting the LLC in a relative's name with no real economic capacity.
   - Consequence: The IRS can ignore the transfer and apply penalties.

**Intentionally not filing Form 5472.**
   - Hiding transactions between the owner and the LLC.
   - Consequence: $25,000 penalty per year.

**Declaring the LLC has no income when it does.**
   - Reporting $0 in income when the LLC received payments.
   - Consequence: IRS audit, penalties, and possible criminal action.

**Using the LLC to hide personal assets from creditors.**
   - Transferring personal assets to the LLC to prevent a creditor from claiming them.
   - Consequence: A judge can reverse the transfer (fraudulent conveyance).

## Gray area: Aggressive tax avoidance

Between planning and evasion there is a **gray area** where some practices use legal loopholes in ways the law does not explicitly prohibit but that were not intended by the legislator.

### Examples of tax avoidance

**Forming the LLC in one state and physically operating in another.**
   - Legal, but if there is no real economic purpose, the IRS may question it.

**Paying yourself a low salary from the LLC to avoid taxes.**
   - Legal if the salary is at market rate; illegal if artificially low.

**Using complex offshore structures with no economic purpose.**
   - If the only reason is to avoid taxes, it may be considered abusive avoidance.

### Risks of aggressive avoidance

- The IRS can apply the **economic substance doctrine**.
- Courts can ignore the structure if it has no real business purpose.
- You may end up paying taxes plus penalties and interest.

> **General rule:** If the structure has no business sense beyond saving taxes, you are in the risk zone.

## Consequences of tax evasion

### For the IRS (U.S.)

- **Civil penalties:** Up to 75% of unpaid taxes
- **Interest:** From the original due date
- **Audit:** Full investigation of your finances
- **Criminal charges:** Up to 5 years in prison for tax fraud
- **LLC closure:** The IRS can revoke the EIN
- **Extradition:** Possible if the amount is significant
### For your country of residence

- **Penalties:** Variable by country (up to 300% of unpaid tax)
- **Criminal charges:** Jail in most countries for tax evasion
- **Account seizure:** In your country and potentially the U.S.
- **Business ban:** Cannot be a director of companies
- **LLC loss:** The state can dissolve the LLC
### Reputational cost

- You will not be able to open bank accounts in the future.
- Banks (Mercury, Relay) will put you on blacklists.
- Difficulty finding investors or partners.
- Problems obtaining visas or U.S. residency.

## How to ensure you are on the right side

### Principles of ethical tax planning

1. **Declare all your income.** Always. In all countries where you have an obligation.
2. **Document everything.** Invoices, contracts, receipts. If it is not documented, it does not exist.
3. **Use structures with real economic purpose.** The LLC must have a genuine business.
4. **Pay what the law requires, no more, no less.** Planning seeks to pay the legal minimum, not evade.
5. **Consult with professionals.** A CPA and a tax attorney are an investment, not an expense.
6. **Review your structure regularly.** Laws change; what is legal today may not be tomorrow.

### Self-assessment questions

- **Do you declare all your LLC income?:** May be evasion
- **Do you have real documentation for your expenses?:** May be evasion
- **Does your LLC have a real business?:** May be abusive avoidance
- **Do you use the LLC bank account only for business expenses?:** Mixing finances = risk
- **Do you file all forms required by the IRS?:** Non-compliance = penalties
- **Do you know how your country treats your LLC for tax purposes?:** Ignorance = risk

## Common myths

### Myth 1: "If the LLC doesn't pay taxes in the U.S., I don't have to declare anything"

**Reality:** The LLC does not pay U.S. taxes (if it is a disregarded entity without ECI), but **you** must declare the income in your country of residence.

### Myth 2: "The LLC is a structure to avoid paying taxes"

**Reality:** The LLC is a legal structure to **organize your business**, not to evade taxes. LLC income is taxed in your country of residence.

### Myth 3: "If the accountant says it, it's legal"

**Reality:** Not all accountants know international taxation. Bad advice can lead you to evasion without knowing it.

### Myth 4: "As long as I'm not audited, I'm fine"

**Reality:** The IRS and tax authorities have data cross-referencing systems. The probability of detection increases over time.

### Myth 5: "LLCs are for money laundering"

**Reality:** This myth damages the reputation of those who use LLCs legitimately. LLCs are legal and respected structures worldwide.

## Conclusion

The difference between tax evasion and tax planning is clear:

- **Tax planning:** Use the rules in your favor. It is legal, ethical, and sustainable.
- **Tax evasion:** Break the rules. It is illegal, risky, and has serious consequences.
- **Aggressive avoidance:** Play in the gray area. Not recommended without expert advice.

**The key to staying on the right side:** Declare all your income, document all your transactions, use structures with real economic purpose, and consult with professionals.

It is not worth risking your assets, your freedom, and your reputation to save taxes illegally. Legal tax planning lets you sleep peacefully.
