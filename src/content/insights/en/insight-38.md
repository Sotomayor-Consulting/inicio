In an increasingly globalized world, governments have strengthened international tax cooperation. One of the most important instruments is the **CRS (Common Reporting Standard)** . But **what exactly is CRS and how does it affect you?** Below, we explain everything you need to know about this global standard for automatic exchange of financial information.

## 1. What Is CRS?

The **Common Reporting Standard (CRS)** is an international standard developed by the **Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD)** for the automatic exchange of financial information between countries. Its goal is to combat cross-border tax evasion by identifying taxpayers who hold financial accounts outside their country of tax residence.

### How does it work?
- Financial institutions (banks, investment funds, insurance companies) must identify clients who are **tax residents of other countries**
- These institutions annually report the account information to their **local tax authority**
- The local tax authority automatically exchanges that information with the client's **country of tax residence**

## 2. CRS vs. FATCA: Key Differences

Although CRS and FATCA pursue similar goals, there are key differences:

- **Organization:** OECD (multilateral) -- U.S. (unilateral)
- **Participation:** Over 100 countries -- U.S. and countries with IGA
- **Exchange type:** Automatic and multilateral -- Automatic and bilateral
- **Legal basis:** Multilateral Competent Authority Agreement (MCAA) -- Intergovernmental Agreements (IGA)
- **Thresholds:** Generally none (low-value preexisting accounts may be exempt) -- Vary by form
- **Information reported:** Balances, income (interest, dividends), financial products -- Similar + account holders
- **U.S. participation:** U.S. does not participate in CRS (has its own FATCA system) -- Mandatory for global financial institutions
> **Key fact:** The U.S. has **not** adopted CRS. Instead, it uses FATCA to receive information from other countries but does not automatically exchange information under CRS.

## 3. What Information Is Exchanged Under CRS?

Financial institutions must report the following information for each reportable account:

### Account holder information:
- Full name
- Address
- Date and place of birth
- Country(ies) of tax residence
- Tax Identification Number (TIN)

### Account information:
- Account number
- Name and identification number of the financial institution
- Account balance or value at year-end
- For custodial accounts: gross dividends, interest, and other income
- For depository accounts: gross interest paid
- For other accounts: gross amount paid or credited

## 4. What Accounts Are Subject to Reporting?

CRS applies to a wide range of financial accounts:

### Reportable accounts:
- **Depository accounts**: Checking, savings, certificates of deposit
- **Custodial accounts**: Accounts holding stocks, bonds, investment funds
- **Equity interests in investment entities**: Mutual funds, hedge funds, private equity
- **Cash value insurance contracts**: Life insurance with investment component
- **Annuity contracts**

### Excluded accounts:
- Retirement accounts meeting certain requirements
- Accounts with a balance under $250,000 (for preexisting individual accounts in some cases)
- Certain low-risk accounts

## 5. Who Does CRS Affect?

CRS affects:

### Affected persons:
- **Individuals** who are tax residents of one country and hold financial accounts in another country
- **Entities** (companies, trusts, foundations) controlled by persons from another country
- **Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs)** and their relatives (due to enhanced due diligence)

### Practical examples:
- A tax resident of Mexico with a bank account in Switzerland → Switzerland reports to Mexico
- A tax resident of Colombia with investments in Spain → Spain reports to Colombia
- A tax resident of Brazil with an account in Portugal → Portugal reports to Brazil

## 6. Due Diligence Procedure

Financial institutions must follow a due diligence process to identify reportable accounts:

### For new accounts:
- **Self-certification**: The account holder must declare their country(ies) of tax residence (similar to W-8BEN or W-9)
- The institution verifies the reasonableness of the self-certification

### For preexisting accounts:
- **Low-value accounts** (balance ≤ $250,000): Electronic search for foreign residency indicators
- **High-value accounts** (balance > $250,000): Electronic + paper search + contacting the account holder if needed

### Foreign residency indicators:
- Address in another country
- Phone number in another country
- Transfer instructions to another country
- Power of attorney to someone with an address in another country
- Place of birth in another country

## 7. Participating Countries in CRS

Over **100 countries** currently participate in CRS, including:

### Americas:
- Argentina, Brazil, Canada, Chile, Colombia, Costa Rica, Ecuador, Mexico, Panama, Paraguay, Peru, Uruguay

### Europe:
- All EU countries (Spain, Portugal, France, Germany, Italy, etc.), United Kingdom, Switzerland, Norway, Liechtenstein, Monaco

### Asia and Pacific:
- Australia, New Zealand, Japan, South Korea, Singapore, Hong Kong, India, China, Indonesia, Malaysia

### Middle East and Africa:
- United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, South Africa, Mauritius, Seychelles

### Tax havens and financial centers:
- Cayman Islands, British Virgin Islands, Bermuda, Bahamas, Panama, Singapore, Hong Kong

> **Note:** The list of participating countries is constantly updated. Some countries have deferred implementation timelines.

## 8. Penalties for Non-Compliance

Consequences of not reporting accounts under CRS vary by country but generally include:

### For taxpayers:
- **Fines** for not declaring foreign accounts (significant in many countries)
- **Interest** on unpaid taxes
- **Potential criminal charges** for tax evasion
- **Extended statute of limitations** for tax offenses

### For financial institutions:
- **Administrative penalties** for non-compliance with due diligence procedures
- **Reputational sanctions**
- **Possible license revocation** in serious cases

## 9. CRS and U.S. LLC Owners

If you are a tax resident of a country outside the U.S. and own a **U.S. LLC**, you should know that:

- The U.S. **does not participate in CRS**, so the IRS does not automatically exchange information under this standard
- However, your LLC information may be reported to the IRS under U.S. tax rules
- Your country of residence may request information from the U.S. under **Double Taxation Treaties** or **Tax Information Exchange Agreements (TIEA)**
- As a U.S. LLC owner, you must comply with tax obligations **in both the U.S. and your country of residence**

> **Important:** Although the U.S. does not participate in CRS, the global information exchange network has expanded significantly, making tax opacity increasingly difficult.

## 10. Recommendations for CRS Compliance

### Practical steps:
1. **Know your country(ies) of tax residence**: Determine where you are a tax resident under each country's laws
2. **Correctly declare your tax residence** at all your financial institutions
3. **Provide accurate self-certifications** when requested
4. **Review your foreign accounts** and ensure they are properly reported on your local tax return
5. **Consult an international tax specialist** to ensure compliance in all countries where you have tax obligations
