**Running an LLC comes with ongoing compliance obligations that many business owners overlook until it is too late.** Unlike a sole proprietorship, an LLC is a separate legal entity that requires regular maintenance to preserve its liability protection and good standing. Missing a single filing can trigger penalties, loss of good standing, and even administrative dissolution.

The good news is that LLC compliance is manageable when you break it down into a year-round system. Most tasks take 10-30 minutes and cost less than $200 per year. The key is knowing what to do, when to do it, and having a system to track it all.

In this guide, we provide a **complete annual compliance checklist for LLCs in 2026**: federal compliance requirements, state annual reports and franchise taxes, registered agent maintenance, business license renewals, multi-state obligations, a month-by-month compliance calendar, a compliance score tool, and tips for staying on track year after year.

## 1. Federal Compliance Requirements

### IRS Annual Filing Requirements

| Requirement | Deadline | Applies To | Penalty for Missing |
|-------------|----------|------------|-------------------|
| Form 1065 (Partnership LLC) | March 15 | Multi-member LLCs taxed as partnerships | $220 per month per partner |
| Form 1120-S (S-Corp LLC) | March 15 | LLCs taxed as S corporations | $220 per month per shareholder |
| Form 1120 (C-Corp LLC) | April 15 | LLCs taxed as C corporations | 5% of tax per month (max 25%) |
| Schedule C (1040) | April 15 | Single-member LLCs (disregarded entity) | 5% of tax per month (max 25%) |
| Form 941 (Payroll) | Quarterly | LLCs with employees | 2-15% of payroll tax |
| Form 940 (FUTA) | January 31 | LLCs with employees | Varies |
| Form W-2 / W-3 | January 31 | LLCs with employees | $60-$310 per form |
| Form 1099-NEC | January 31 | LLCs that paid contractors $600+ | $60-$310 per form |
| FinCEN BOI Report | Varies (see below) | Most LLCs (new rules for 2026) | $591 per day (civil) / up to $10,000 (criminal) |

### Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) Report

As of 2024, the Corporate Transparency Act requires most LLCs to file a Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) report with FinCEN. In 2026, this requirement is fully in effect:

| Entity Type | Initial Filing Deadline | Update Deadline |
|------------|----------------------|----------------|
| LLCs created before 2024 | January 1, 2025 (passed) | Within 30 days of any change |
| LLCs created in 2024 | 90 days from formation | Within 30 days of any change |
| LLCs created in 2025 | 30 days from formation | Within 30 days of any change |
| LLCs created in 2026 | 30 days from formation | Within 30 days of any change |

**What you need to file:**
- Full legal name, date of birth, and address of each beneficial owner (25%+ ownership or substantial control)
- A unique identifying number (passport, driver's license, or FinCEN ID) for each owner
- An image of the identifying document
- Company information: legal name, trade names, business address, EIN, jurisdiction of formation

### EIN Maintenance

- Keep your EIN confirmation letter (CP 575) in your business records
- Update your EIN information with the IRS if the responsible party changes
- Close your EIN only if the LLC is formally dissolved (do not abandon it)
- File final tax returns when dissolving the LLC

### California Note

California requires two separate compliance obligations:
1. **Statement of Information** ($20) — filed with the SOS every 2 years (biennial for corps, annual for LLCs)
2. **Minimum Franchise Tax** ($800) — paid annually to the Franchise Tax Board (FTB), regardless of income

The $800 minimum franchise tax applies even if the LLC has no income or is inactive. New LLCs are exempt for the first year. Failure to pay results in penalties, interest, and eventual suspension.

### Delaware Note

Delaware LLCs that do not have income or do not conduct business in Delaware pay no franchise tax and no annual report fee. However, Delaware LLCs that are corporations or that elect corporate taxation must pay a minimum franchise tax of $300 and file an annual report.

## 3. Registered Agent Compliance

Every LLC must maintain a registered agent in its state of formation and in any state where it is foreign qualified. The registered agent must have a physical street address in the state (no PO Boxes).

### Registered Agent Checklist

- [ ] Confirm registered agent name and address on file with the SOS are current
- [ ] Verify the agent has accepted the appointment (written consent on file)
- [ ] Check that the agent's physical address is correct (not just a PO Box)
- [ ] Update the agent within 30 days if the agent resigns or changes address
- [ ] Review agent service fees annually — commercial agents typically charge $50-$300 per year per state
- [ ] Designate an agent in each state where the LLC is foreign qualified
- [ ] Confirm the agent forwards legal notices and state correspondence promptly

### Consequences of Lapsed Registered Agent

| Consequence | Timeline | Impact |
|------------|----------|--------|
| State notices go unanswered | Immediately | Missed compliance reminders and legal summons |
| Default judgment in lawsuits | 20-60 days | Personal liability for legal claims |
| Loss of good standing | 30-90 days | Cannot conduct business or obtain financing |
| Administrative dissolution | 6-12 months | Entity terminated by the state |
| Name becomes available | 1-3 years | Business name can be taken by others |

## 5. Multi-State Compliance (Foreign Qualification)

If your LLC operates in multiple states, you may need to foreign qualify in each state where you have nexus. Nexus is created by:

- Having a physical presence (office, warehouse, employees)
- Exceeding economic nexus thresholds (typically $100K-$500K in sales)
- Owning property in the state
- Having a registered agent or bank account in the state (varies by state)
- Meeting clients in the state regularly

### Multi-State Compliance Checklist

- [ ] Identify all states where the LLC has nexus
- [ ] File foreign qualification in each nexus state
- [ ] Designate a registered agent in each foreign state
- [ ] File annual reports in each state of qualification
- [ ] Pay franchise tax in each state that requires it
- [ ] File state income tax returns in each nexus state
- [ ] Collect and remit sales tax in each state where you have sales tax nexus
- [ ] Monitor economic nexus thresholds (they change frequently)

## 9. Common Compliance Gaps and How to Avoid Them

| Gap | Why It Happens | Prevention |
|-----|---------------|------------|
| Missed annual report | Anniversary-based deadlines shift each year | Set calendar reminders 60 and 30 days before |
| Unpaid franchise tax | Business owner thinks "no income = no tax" | Understand state rules — CA $800 applies regardless |
| Lapsed registered agent | Agent resigns without owner knowing | Use a commercial registered agent service |
| Outdated SOS address | Business moved without updating state | Update SOS records immediately upon moving |
| Missed BOI filing | New requirement, many owners unaware | File within 30 days of formation; update within 30 days of changes |
| Unfiled state tax return | Owner files only federal return | Check state filing requirements for your entity type |
| Expired business license | Local renewal not tracked | Add local renewals to your compliance calendar |
| Mixed personal/business funds | No separate bank account | Open a dedicated business bank account immediately |
