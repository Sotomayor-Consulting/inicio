The **Canadian Limited Partnership (LP)** has become a popular structure for international investors and entrepreneurs, especially those looking to operate in North America with unique tax advantages. But **what are the benefits of a Canadian LP and why might it be the right structure for you?**

Below, we explain everything you need to know about this corporate vehicle, its advantages, limitations, and key considerations.

## 1. What Is a Canadian Limited Partnership (LP)?

A **Limited Partnership (LP)** is a partnership composed of two types of partners:

### General Partner (GP):
- Manages and administers the partnership
- Has **unlimited liability** for the LP's debts
- Typically a corporation (to limit its liability)

### Limited Partner (LP):
- Contributes capital but does not participate in management
- Has **limited liability** to the amount of their investment
- Receives profit distributions

### Key characteristics:
- It is a "pass-through entity" (does not pay tax at the entity level)
- Profits and losses flow directly to partners
- Governed by Canadian provincial law (most commonly Alberta, Ontario, or British Columbia)

## 2. Key Benefit: Not a Taxable Entity in Canada

The most important benefit of a Canadian LP for non-residents is its **tax treatment**.

### For non-resident partners:
- If the Limited Partner **does not carry on business in Canada**, the LP is not required to file a Canadian tax return
- LP profits are considered **foreign-source income** for the non-resident partner
- No Canadian withholding tax on distributions to the Limited Partner (under certain conditions)

### Why is this relevant?
This means a non-resident can invest through a Canadian LP and **have no Canadian tax obligations**, provided:

- The limited partner does not actively participate in management
- The LP does not carry on business in Canada
- The partner is not a Canadian tax resident

## 3. Vehicle for Investing in the U.S. from Canada

A Canadian LP can be used as a vehicle to invest in the United States with tax advantages:

### Typical structure for non-U.S. investors:
```
Foreign Investor
         ↓ (Limited Partner)
Canadian LP (invests in the U.S.)
         ↓
U.S. LLC or other U.S. assets
```

### Advantages for global investors:
- The investor avoids creating a direct U.S. structure
- The Canadian LP acts as an intermediate tax "shield"
- Canada-U.S. tax treaties offer reduced rates
- Facilitates investment in U.S. real estate, startups, and financial markets

## 4. Privacy and Confidentiality

Canada offers certain corporate privacy advantages:

### What is protected?
- Limited Partner names are **not publicly registered** in most provinces
- Only the General Partner appears in public records
- LP financial information is private (not filed publicly)

### Comparison with the U.S.:
| Aspect | Canadian LP | U.S. LLC |
|---|---|---|
| Partner registration | Private (LP not registered) | Generally private in most states |
| Financial information | Private | Private in most states |
| Beneficial ownership reporting | Federal reporting (since 2024) | BOI (Corporate Transparency Act) |

> **Important note:** Since 2024, Canada is implementing a federal beneficial ownership registry, which reduces anonymity. However, it remains more private than many structures in other jurisdictions.

## 5. Flexibility in Profit Distribution

The LP offers great flexibility in how profits are distributed:

### Features:
- Distributions **do not have to be proportional** to capital contributions
- Different **classes of partnership interests** with different rights can be created
- Losses can be allocated to specific partners
- Terms are defined in the **Limited Partnership Agreement**

### Example distribution structure:
- Preferred Return: 8% for the Limited Partner before the GP receives anything
- 80/20 split after preferred return
- GP catch-up after a certain threshold

## 6. Tax Efficiency for Real Estate Investments

The Canadian LP is especially popular for real estate investments:

### Advantages for real estate:
- **No Canadian tax** on capital gains if the non-resident LP does not carry on business in Canada
- Properties can be held in the LP's name without creating Canadian tax presence
- Facilitates investment in U.S. commercial real estate
- Allows pooling capital from multiple investors

### Typical structure:
```
Canadian LP (investment vehicle)
         ↓
U.S. LLC (property owner)
         ↓
U.S. real estate property
```

## 7. Formation and Maintenance Costs

Compared to other international structures, the Canadian LP is relatively inexpensive:

### Estimated costs:
| Concept | Cost |
|---|---|
| Formation (provincial registration) | $200 - $500 CAD |
| Registered office (annual) | $200 - $400 CAD |
| Annual return (provincial) | $100 - $300 CAD |
| Accounting and tax (annual) | $1,000 - $3,000 CAD |
| **Total annual maintenance** | **$1,300 - $3,700+ CAD** |

### Advantage over European holdings:
- Significantly cheaper than a Netherlands, Luxembourg, or Switzerland holding
- No mandatory external audit requirements
- No local director requirements (only the GP needs to be in Canada)

## 8. Legal Requirements and Considerations

### To set up a Canadian LP:

**Requirements:**
- At least one **General Partner** (individual or corporation resident in Canada)
- At least one **Limited Partner** (can be a non-resident)
- **Limited Partnership Agreement** (founding document)
- Provincial registration in the province of formation

**Ongoing obligations:**
- Annual Return (provincial)
- Cannot have more than 50 partners (in some provinces)
- GP must maintain presence in Canada
- LP must not participate in management (or loses limited liability)

### Risk to avoid:
> If a Limited Partner participates in the management of the LP, they **lose limited liability** and may be treated as a General Partner with unlimited liability.

| Aspect | Canadian LP | U.S. LLC | European Holding |

| **Annual cost** | $1,300 - $3,700 CAD | $500 - $2,000 USD | $9,500 - $54,000+ |

| **Non-resident taxation** | Favorable (if no business in Canada) | Depends on state | Depends on country |
| **Treaty access** | Canada-U.S. favorable | U.S. treaty network | Country's treaty network |
| **Asset protection** | High (LP) | High (LLC) | High |
| **Complexity** | Medium | Low-Medium | High |

## 10. Who Is a Canadian LP Ideal For?

### Ideal for:
- **International investors** wanting to invest in the U.S. from a Canadian structure
- **Families** seeking privacy and asset protection
- **Commercial real estate investors** in the U.S.
- **Private equity and venture capital firms** pooling capital from multiple investors
- **Canadian non-residents** wanting a tax-efficient structure without creating Canadian tax presence

### NOT ideal for:
- **Canadian residents** (lose many tax advantages)
- **Operating businesses** within Canada
- **People wanting to actively manage** the investment (risk of losing limited liability)
- **Those seeking a structure without a Canadian GP** (mandatory requirement)

## Conclusion

The **benefits of a Canadian LP** make it an attractive structure for international investors, especially those looking to invest in the U.S. from a tax-efficient jurisdiction. Its favorable tax treatment for non-residents, privacy features, and flexibility in profit distribution are advantages hard to find in other structures.

However, it is not a universal solution. The need for a Canadian General Partner, restrictions on Limited Partner participation, and regulatory changes in transparency must be carefully considered.
