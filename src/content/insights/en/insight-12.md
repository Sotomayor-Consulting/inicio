Does an LLC pay taxes? The answer is not an automatic yes or no. It depends on several factors.

What determines whether an LLC pays taxes:

- Who owns the LLC.
- Whether it has one or multiple members.
- Whether the owner is a U.S. tax resident or non-resident.
- Where the business activity takes place.
- Whether the income is from U.S. sources.
- Whether there is physical presence, employees, office, or inventory in the U.S.
- Whether the LLC elected to be taxed as a corporation.

## Case 1: Single-member foreign-owned LLC

A single-member LLC owned by a non-resident alien can typically be treated as a **disregarded entity** for tax purposes.

This means the LLC can be "transparent" for income tax purposes. However, if owned by a foreign person, it may have an obligation to file Form 5472 together with a pro forma Form 1120 when there are reportable transactions with related parties. The IRS includes foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entities within these reporting entities.

## Case 2: LLC with two or more members

When an LLC has multiple members, it is generally treated as a partnership for tax purposes. In that case, it must typically file Form 1065, which is an informational return reporting income, expenses, gains, losses, and deductions. The IRS clarifies that a partnership files an informational return but does not pay income tax directly at the entity level.

Members receive a Schedule K-1, which reports each member's share of the partnership items.

## Case 3: LLC generating U.S.-source income

If the LLC has income effectively connected with a U.S. trade or business (Effectively Connected Income or ECI), there may be an obligation to pay federal taxes and file additional returns.

This can occur, for example, if the company has:

- A physical office in the U.S.
- Employees in the U.S.
- Services physically performed within the U.S.
- Inventory or logistics within the country.
- Active commercial operations in U.S. territory.

## Case 4: LLC that elected to be taxed as a Corporation

An LLC can make a tax election to be taxed as a corporation. In that case, the company is no longer treated as a transparent entity and may be subject to corporate taxes.

This decision must be carefully analyzed, as it can completely change the company's tax treatment.

## So, does an LLC always pay taxes?

Not necessarily.

An LLC can have three different scenarios:

1. **No federal income tax payment**, but must file informational forms.
2. **Pays taxes in the U.S.**, if it generates income subject to U.S. taxation.
3. **Has state obligations**, even if no federal tax is due.

## State obligations

In addition to the IRS, each state may require reports, fees, or annual taxes.

For example, an LLC may be required to pay:

- Annual Report.
- Franchise Tax.
- Registered Agent.
- State maintenance fees.

These obligations exist even if the LLC has not generated income.

## Common mistake: thinking an LLC equals zero taxes

An LLC **is not a magic tool** to avoid paying taxes. The tax treatment depends on the specific case. Opening an LLC without planning can lead to:

- Fines.
- Unfiled forms.
- Banking problems.
- IRS risks.
- Double taxation or poor structuring.

An LLC can be an excellent tool for Latin American entrepreneurs, but it must be structured correctly.

## The right question

The question should not only be: Does an LLC pay taxes?

The right question is: **based on my activity, tax residency, and business model, what obligations would my LLC have before the IRS and the state?**

## Summary table

| Scenario | Pays federal tax? | Files forms? |
|----------|-----------------|-------------|
| Single-member foreign LLC without ECI | No | Yes (5472, 1120 pro forma) |
| Multi-member LLC without ECI | No (pass-through) | Yes (1065, K-1) |
| LLC with ECI (U.S. income) | Yes | Yes |
| LLC electing C-Corp taxation | Yes | Yes |

## Conclusion

An LLC does not automatically pay taxes, but it is not exempt from obligations either. The tax treatment will depend on its structure, activity, income, and tax residency.

The best approach is to analyze each case with professional advice before forming the LLC, to avoid tax contingencies and take advantage of the real benefits of this structure.
