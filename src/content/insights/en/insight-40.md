When you conduct business operations internationally — whether as a freelancer, entrepreneur, or investor — **proper documentation** is not just a good practice: it is a tax obligation. Knowing **how to document international operations** correctly protects you during audits, facilitates tax compliance, and avoids penalties.

Below, we present a complete guide on the records and documents you must maintain for each type of international operation.

## 1. International Contracts and Agreements

Everything starts with a contract. A well-drafted agreement is the foundation of a properly documented international operation.

### Essential elements of an international contract:
- **Complete party information**: Legal name, address, country of tax residence, EIN or TIN
- **Service or product description**: Clear detail of scope, deliverables, and specifications
- **Payment terms**: Currency, payment method, deadlines, late penalties
- **Tax clause**: Withholding responsibility, applicable tax treaties, tax residence declaration
- **Governing law and jurisdiction**: Which country's law governs and where disputes will be resolved
- **Dates**: Signing date, start date, end date, and milestones

### Common contract types:
- **Service Agreement**: For professional services
- **Independent Contractor Agreement**: For freelancers
- **Distribution Agreement**: For product distribution
- **License Agreement**: For intellectual property use
- **NDA (Non-Disclosure Agreement)**: To protect confidential information

> **Recommendation:** All contracts should be in **languages both parties understand** and preferably translated by a certified translator if in different languages.

## 2. International Invoicing

International invoices must meet specific requirements to be tax-valid.

### Elements of an international invoice:
- **Unique and sequential invoice number**
- **Issue date**
- **Complete issuer information**: Name, address, EIN or tax ID, business registration if applicable
- **Complete client information**: Name, address, tax ID or VAT number
- **Detailed description**: Service provided or product sold, quantities, unit prices
- **Currency**: Clearly indicate the transaction currency
- **Total amount**: In the transaction currency and, if applicable, its USD equivalent
- **Payment terms**: Due date, accepted payment methods
- **Banking instructions**: For international transfers (SWIFT, IBAN, routing number)
- **Tax notes**: If withholding tax applies, treaty claims, exemptions

### Accepted formats:
- PDF invoice (recommended)
- Electronic invoice (per each country's requirements)
- Paper invoice (with physical copy)

## 3. International Payment Records

Documenting the flow of money is crucial. You must keep:

### Types of payment records:
- **Bank transfers**: SWIFT/MT103, transfer confirmations
- **PayPal, Wise, Payoneer**: Transaction receipts, account statements
- **Cryptocurrencies**: Transaction hash, wallet record, USD value at transaction time
- **International checks**: Copy of front and back
- **Bank drafts**: Issuing bank receipt

### Information each record should contain:
- Transaction date
- Amount in original currency and in USD
- Sender and beneficiary name
- Reference or confirmation number
- Transaction description (linked to invoice)

## 4. Withholding Tax Documentation

If you conduct operations subject to withholding tax, you must document:

### For the payer (if you withhold taxes):
- **Form W-8BEN** (foreign individual provider)
- **Form W-8BEN-E** (foreign entity provider)
- **Form W-9** (U.S. provider)
- **Form 1042-S** (annual withholding report to foreign persons)
- **Deposit receipt** of withheld taxes to the IRS

### For the recipient (if taxes are withheld from you):
- **Certificate of tax residence** issued by your local tax authority
- **Form W-8BEN** submitted to the payer
- **Withholding certificate** (Certificate of Withholding, local 1042-S)
- **Treaty rate reduction request** (if applicable)

## 5. Exchange Rate Records

When transactions are in foreign currency, you must document the exchange rate used.

### IRS-accepted methods:
- **Spot exchange rate** on transaction date (reliable source)
- **Monthly or annual average rate** (published by the IRS)
- **Transaction rate** (applied by your bank or payment processor)

### Recommended documentation:
- Screenshot of the rate used (with source and date)
- Bank statement showing the conversion applied
- Internal record with documented rate for each transaction
- Tools: OANDA, XE.com, IRS Annual Average Exchange Rates

### Suggested record:
- **01/15/2025:** Invoice #001 -- EUR -- 1,000.00 -- 1.03 -- 1,030.00 -- OANDA
## 6. International Expense Documentation

If you deduct expenses related to international operations, you must document:

### Travel expenses:
- Airfare (boarding pass + receipt)
- Hotels (detailed invoice)
- Meals (receipts, maximum 50% deductible)
- Local transportation (receipts)
- **Trip purpose**: Agenda, emails, related contracts

### Communication expenses:
- Internet and phone bills (with international call details)
- Communication tool subscriptions (Zoom, Slack, Teams)

### Legal and professional expenses:
- Attorney, accountant, consultant invoices
- Contracts and service scope

### International marketing expenses:
- Google Ads, Facebook Ads, LinkedIn Ads invoices
- Contracts with influencers or foreign agencies
- Campaign reports

## 7. Corporate Structure Records

If you operate through entities in multiple countries:

### Documents to maintain:
- **Certificate of incorporation** for each entity
- **EIN or tax ID** for each entity
- **Operating Agreement / Bylaws**
- **Membership or shareholder register**
- **Meeting minutes** and corporate resolutions
- **Intercompany agreements** between related entities

### For U.S. LLC structures:
- Articles of Organization
- Operating Agreement
- EIN confirmation letter (CP 575)
- Registered agent agreement
- Annual Report filings
- State fee payment receipts

## 8. Audit Preparation Documentation

In case of an IRS or other tax authority audit, you must have:

### Audit file preparation:
- **Chronological index** of all operations
- **Complete contracts and agreements**
- **Invoices and payment records**
- **Correspondence** with clients, suppliers, and authorities
- **Accounting records** (Balance Sheet, Income Statement)
- **Tax returns** filed in all countries
- **Tax payment receipts**

### Transfer pricing documentation:
If you conduct transactions with related parties abroad, you need:
- **Transfer pricing study**
- **Documented intercompany agreements**
- **Functional analysis**
- **Benchmarking** of comparable transactions

## 9. Digital Asset and Cryptocurrency Records

If your international operations involve cryptocurrencies:

### Required documentation:
- **Transaction log**: Date, amount, trading pair, USD rate, hash
- **Exchange records**: Statements from Binance, Coinbase, Kraken, etc.
- **Wallet records**: Public addresses, purpose of each wallet
- **NFT documentation**: Smart contracts, purchase/sale value
- **Staking and mining**: Reward records, USD value when received

### Recommended tools:
- CoinTracking, Koinly, CryptoTrader.Tax
- Custom spreadsheets (with USD conversion formula)
