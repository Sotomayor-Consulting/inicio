More and more freelancers, consultants, agencies, and professionals from Latin America are using an LLC in the United States to sell their services worldwide. And it is no coincidence: a well-structured LLC removes many of the barriers faced by those trying to get paid internationally from Latin America.

In this article, we will explain step by step how to use your LLC to sell international services, from banking setup to invoicing and tax compliance.

## Why use an LLC to sell international services?

An LLC allows you to operate as a U.S. company in the eyes of your clients, banks, and payment platforms. This is key because:

- **International clients** prefer to hire formal companies.
- **Payment platforms** like Stripe, PayPal Business, and Transferwise require or favor U.S.-registered entities.
- **Banks and fintechs** open dollar accounts without the restrictions applied to individuals.
- **The perception of trust** increases significantly when invoicing from an LLC.

## Step 1: Form your LLC in the right state

The first step is choosing the state to incorporate your LLC. For international service businesses, the most recommended states are:

| State | Advantage | Annual cost |
|-------|-----------|-------------|
| **Wyoming** | Privacy, low cost, no state tax | $60 |
| **Florida** | Proximity to LATAM, business image | $138.75 |
| **Delaware** | Ideal if seeking investment | $300 |

**Recommendation:** If your business is 100% digital with no physical U.S. operations, Wyoming is usually the best choice for its low maintenance costs and privacy.

## Step 2: Obtain the EIN

The EIN (Employer Identification Number) is your LLC's tax number issued by the IRS. You will need it to:

- Open business bank accounts.
- Set up Stripe, PayPal Business, and other platforms.
- Invoice clients.
- File informational returns.

> **Important:** As a non-resident alien, you can obtain your EIN without needing an SSN or Green Card.

## Step 3: Open a business bank account

You will need a bank account in your LLC's name to receive payments from your clients. The best options for Latin Americans are:

- **Mercury:** 100% online, no physical presence required, ideal for startups and digital businesses.
- **Relay:** Business accounts with multiple users, ideal for agencies.
- **Wise Business:** Perfect for receiving international payments and converting currencies.
- **Novo:** Good option for freelancers and small businesses.

### What do you need to open the account?

- LLC's Certificate of Formation.
- EIN Letter from the IRS.
- Operating Agreement.
- Valid passport.
- Personal address in your country of residence.

## Step 4: Set up payment platforms

Once you have your LLC and bank account, you can set up payment platforms to charge your clients:

### Stripe

- Accepts credit card, debit, and local payment methods.
- Integrates with most invoicing platforms.
- Transfers are deposited directly into your business bank account.

### PayPal Business

- Allows you to send professional invoices.
- Accepts international payments in multiple currencies.
- Offers seller protection.

### Wise

- Ideal for receiving payments from clients in different currencies.
- Competitive conversion rates.
- Integrates with your U.S. bank account.

## Step 5: Invoice your international clients

With your active LLC, you can invoice your clients as a U.S. company. Your invoice should include:

- Legal name of the LLC.
- LLC registered address.
- EIN.
- Invoice number.
- Service description.
- Amount in dollars.
- Payment terms.

### Recommended invoicing tools

- **FreshBooks:** Ideal for freelancers and consultants.
- **Wave:** Free, good for small businesses.
- **Stripe Invoicing:** Integrates directly with your payment processor.
- **QuickBooks:** Standard for business accounting.

## Step 6: Tax compliance for service exporters

One of the most frequent questions is: what taxes do I need to pay if I sell services through my LLC?

### If you have no physical presence in the U.S. and your clients are outside the U.S.

In many cases, the LLC does not owe federal income tax in the U.S., but must still file informational forms such as:

- **Form 5472** (if single-member LLC owned by a foreigner).
- **Form 1120 pro forma**.
- **Annual Report** in the state of registration.

### If you provide services to clients within the U.S.

You may have U.S.-source income (ECI) and be subject to federal taxes. In this case, specialized accounting advice is essential.

> **Important:** Always consult with an accountant specializing in international taxation to understand your specific obligations based on your country of residence and type of service.

## Common mistakes when using an LLC to sell services

### Mixing personal and business income

This is the most serious mistake. Your LLC must have its own bank account and its own financial records. Mixing personal and business expenses can lead to tax problems and put your limited liability protection at risk.

### Not invoicing correctly

A professional invoice builds trust. Not invoicing or doing it informally can make clients not take you seriously or cause problems with your accounting records.

### Ignoring annual obligations

Your LLC requires maintenance: Annual Report, registered agent, informational filings. Ignoring these obligations can lead to your company being suspended.

### Not having a service contract

Always use a service contract with your international clients. This protects both your LLC and yourself, and clearly establishes the terms of service, payment method, and dispute resolution.
