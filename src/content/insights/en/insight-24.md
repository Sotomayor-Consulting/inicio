If your LLC has two or more members, the IRS requires you to file **Form 1065** each year. This information return reports the LLC's income, expenses, gains, and losses, and distributes those amounts among the partners through the **Schedule K-1**.

In this guide, we explain what Form 1065 is, who must file it, how to prepare it, and what you need to know to keep your multi-member LLC in compliance.

## What is Form 1065?

Form 1065 is the annual information return that **partnerships** must file with the IRS. Even though your LLC is a limited liability company, if it has more than one member, the IRS treats it as a partnership for tax purposes.

**Key characteristics:**

- It is an **information return**, not a tax return.
- The LLC itself **does not pay taxes** on its profits.
- Profits or losses "pass through" to the partners, who report them on their personal returns.
- Each partner receives a **Schedule K-1** detailing their share.

> **Important:** Form 1065 is due **March 15** (not April 15 like Form 5472). There is no automatic extension beyond September 15.

## Who must file?

You must file Form 1065 if:

1. Your LLC has **two or more members**.
2. You did not elect for your LLC to be taxed as an **S-Corp or C-Corp**.
3. Your LLC had **business activity** during the tax year (income, expenses, investments, etc.).

### What if the LLC had no income?

Even so, if the LLC had expenses, investments, or transactions, it generally must file Form 1065. Even an inactive LLC may need to file depending on the circumstances.

## Components of Form 1065

- **Page 1:** General LLC information: name, EIN, address, formation date
- **Schedule A:** Cost of goods sold (if applicable)
- **Schedule B:** Information about LLC ownership
- **Schedule K:** LLC-level totals of income, expenses, credits, and deductions
- **Schedule M-1:** Reconciliation of book vs tax income
- **Schedule M-2:** Analysis of partners' capital accounts
- **Schedule K-1:** Each partner's individual share of income, expenses, credits, and deductions
## Schedule K-1: The key document for each partner

The **Schedule K-1** is the most important document for multi-member LLC partners. Each partner receives one, containing:

### Information included in the K-1

- **Box 1:** Ordinary business income (loss)
- **Box 2:** Net rental real estate income
- **Box 3:** Other net rental income
- **Box 4:** Interest income
- **Box 5a:** Ordinary dividends
- **Box 5b:** Qualified dividends
- **Box 6:** Royalties
- **Box 7:** Other income
- **Box 8:** Section 199A deduction
- **Box 9-13:** Deductions, credits, and other information
### What does the partner do with the K-1?

Each partner must report the information from their K-1 on their personal tax return:

- **U.S. partners:** On their Form 1040.
- **Foreign partners:** On their Form 1040-NR (if they have ECI) or their local return (according to the tax treaty).

## Key deadlines

- **Form 1065** (without extension):** March 15
- **Form 7004** (extension request):** March 15
- **1065 with extension:** September 15
- **K-1 to partners:** Must be issued before partners file their returns
> **Key difference from Form 5472:** Form 1065 is filed with the IRS on a different date and has different extension rules.

## Step-by-step to prepare Form 1065

### Step 1: Gather financial records

- LLC Profit & Loss statement.
- Balance sheet.
- Record of each partner's transactions (contributions, withdrawals, distributions).
- Personal information for each partner (name, address, EIN or SSN, ownership percentage).

### Step 2: Calculate income and expense totals

Determine at the LLC level:

- **Income:** Sales, services, interest, other income
- **Expenses:** Salaries, rent, marketing, software, travel, depreciation
- **Deductions:** Section 179, retirement plans, etc.
- **Credits:** Applicable tax credits
### Step 3: Distribute among partners

According to each partner's ownership percentage (defined in the Operating Agreement), distribute:

- Ordinary income.
- Capital gains and losses.
- Special deductions.
- Credits.

### Step 4: Complete the form

Fill out each section of Form 1065 with the LLC totals and generate individual K-1s.

### Step 5: Sign and file

- Form 1065 must be signed by a **designated partner** (Tax Matters Partner).
- It is filed electronically with the IRS (mandatory if filing more than 10 returns) or by mail.

### Step 6: Deliver K-1s to partners

Each partner must receive their K-1 in time to file their personal return.

## Key differences: 1065 vs 5472

- **Who files?:** Multi-member LLCs -- Single-member LLCs (foreign owner)
- **Return type:** Informational (income/losses) -- Informational (transactions)
- **Deadline:** March 15 -- April 15
- **Extension:** Until September 15 -- Until October 15
- **K-1:** Yes, for each partner -- N/A
- **Penalty for not filing:** $220 per month per partner -- $25,000 per year
- **Complexity:** Medium-High -- Low-Medium
## Obligations for foreign partners

If your multi-member LLC has non-resident foreign partners:

### 1. Form 1065 with foreign partner information

Form 1065 must include information on all partners, including foreign ones. Name, address, and country of tax residence are required.

### 2. Schedule K-1 for foreign partners

Each foreign partner receives a K-1. Depending on whether the LLC has ECI (Effectively Connected Income), the foreign partner may need to file:

- **Form 1040-NR:** If they have ECI.
- **Form 1040-NR with Schedule NEC:** If they have fixed or determinable income.

### 3. Tax withholding (Chapter 3)

If the LLC distributes income to foreign partners, it may be subject to **30%** withholding unless a tax treaty applies.

### 4. EIN for foreign partners

Each foreign partner needs an **ITIN** (Individual Taxpayer Identification Number) or EIN to report their U.S. taxes.

## Common mistakes

### Mistake 1: Not filing Form 1065 because "the LLC had no income"

Even without income, if the LLC had expenses or partners made contributions, Form 1065 generally must be filed.

### Mistake 2: Confusing Form 1065 with Form 5472

They are different forms for different LLC types. Form 1065 is for multi-member; Form 5472 is for single-member with foreign owner.

### Mistake 3: Not issuing K-1s on time

Partners need their K-1 to file their personal returns. Delaying K-1s delays everyone's returns.

### Mistake 4: Ignoring foreign partner obligations

If you have foreign partners, compliance is more complex. Do not assume they do not need to report anything in the U.S.

### Mistake 5: Not updating the Operating Agreement

Form 1065 distributes profits according to ownership percentage. If the Operating Agreement is not up to date, the distribution may be incorrect.

### Mistake 6: Not signing the form

An unsigned Form 1065 is invalid. The Tax Matters Partner must sign.

## Practical case: Digital agency with two partners

**Situation:** Maria and Juan are 50% partners in a digital marketing agency with an LLC in Florida. In 2024:

- **Total income:** $120,000
- **Operating expenses:** $70,000
- **Net profit:** $50,000
- **Each partner's contribution:** $5,000
- **Distribution to each partner:** $20,000
### What the LLC must do

1. Prepare Form 1065 with totals.
2. Generate two K-1s, each with:
   - Ordinary income: $25,000 (50% of $50,000).
   - Capital contribution: $5,000.
   - Distributions: $20,000.
3. File Form 1065 before March 15.
4. Deliver K-1s to Maria and Juan.

### What each partner must do

- **Maria (U.S. citizen):** Reports her K-1 on her Form 1040.
- **Juan (non-resident foreigner):** Reports his K-1 on his Form 1040-NR (if ECI applies) or on his local return. Additionally, 30% withholding may apply if no tax treaty exists.

## Do you need a CPA?

Form 1065 is significantly more complex than Form 5472. For most multi-member LLCs, hiring a CPA is highly recommended.

### Typical costs

- **Form 1065 + K-1 preparation (2 partners):** $800 - $1,500
- **Per additional partner:** $100 - $200
- **Partner personal return preparation:** $300 - $800
- **Year-round advisory:** $100 - $300/month
## Annual checklist

- [ ] Gather LLC financial statements (P&L, Balance Sheet).
- [ ] Confirm each partner's ownership percentage.
- [ ] Record all contributions and distributions for the year.
- [ ] Prepare Form 1065.
- [ ] Generate Schedule K-1 for each partner.
- [ ] Review with each partner that the K-1 information is correct.
- [ ] Sign Form 1065 (Tax Matters Partner).
- [ ] File Form 1065 with the IRS before March 15 (or September 15 with extension).
- [ ] Deliver K-1s to partners.
- [ ] Keep copies of everything.

## Conclusion

Form 1065 is a key tax obligation for multi-member LLCs. Unlike Form 5472 (simple and low-cost), Form 1065 requires more detailed accounting and generally the help of a CPA.

If your LLC has partners, do not ignore this obligation. Penalties add up, and the IRS has systems to detect partnerships that do not file.

**Key tip:** If you are considering adding a partner to your LLC (moving from disregarded entity to partnership), prepare for the additional complexity of Form 1065 and K-1s.
