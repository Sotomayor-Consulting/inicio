IRS Form 5472 is one of the most important —and most ignored— tax requirements for foreign owners of a U.S. LLC. Failure to file can result in penalties of up to **$25,000 per year**.

In this guide, we explain what it is, who must file it, how to fill it out, and how to avoid common mistakes.

## What is Form 5472?

Form 5472 is an information return that the IRS uses to collect information about transactions between certain U.S. corporations and their foreign related parties.

For practical purposes, if you are a **non-resident alien** (no green card, no U.S. citizenship) and you are the sole owner of an LLC, the IRS treats your LLC as a "domestic corporation" for this form's purposes, and you as a "foreign related party".

**It is not an income tax return.** It is an information return. You do not pay additional taxes by filing it. You simply report transactions between your LLC (in the U.S.) and you (abroad).

## Who must file?

You must file Form 5472 if you meet **all** of these conditions:

1. You are a **non-resident alien** of the U.S. (not a citizen, green card holder, or tax resident).
2. You are the **sole owner** of a U.S. LLC (single-member LLC).
3. Your LLC had **reportable transactions** during the tax year (virtually any transaction counts).

### What are "reportable transactions"?

Any transaction between the LLC (U.S. related party) and the owner (foreign related party) must be reported. Examples:

| Transaction | Reportable? |
|-------------|-------------|
| Initial capital contribution | Yes |
| Fund transfer from owner to LLC | Yes |
| Fund withdrawal from LLC to owner | Yes |
| Personal expense paid from LLC | Yes |
| Loan from owner to LLC | Yes |
| Loan from LLC to owner | Yes |
| Service payment from owner to LLC | Yes |
| Asset purchase between owner and LLC | Yes |

> **General rule:** If there was any money movement between you and your LLC during the year, you must file Form 5472.

## Who does NOT need to file?

You do not need to file Form 5472 if:

1. You are a **U.S. citizen or permanent resident** (green card holder).
2. Your LLC has **two or more members** (multi-member LLC) —different rules apply.
3. Your LLC had **no transactions** with the foreign owner during the year (uncommon).
4. You elected for your LLC to be taxed as an **S-Corp or C-Corp** (different rules apply).

## Filing deadlines

| Event | Deadline |
|--------|---------|
| Standard deadline | April 15 |
| Automatic extension | October 15 |
| Additional extension (with justification) | Possible but not automatic |

Form 5472 is filed together with **Form 1120** (pro-forma). Even if the LLC does not pay taxes, it must file the 1120 as a vehicle for the 5472.

> **IMPORTANT:** There is no automatic extension for the 5472 alone. The extension to October 15 applies if you file Form 7004 to extend the 1120.

## Penalties for not filing

| Violation | Penalty |
|-----------|---------|
| Failure to file 5472 | $25,000 per year |
| Incomplete or incorrect filing | $25,000 per year |
| Failure to file after IRS notice | $25,000 per additional year |
| Late filing without valid extension | $25,000 |

The penalty applies **for each tax year** not filed. If you have not filed for 3 years, the potential penalty is $75,000.

> **Important note:** The IRS has intensified scrutiny of Form 5472 filings in recent years. More and more foreign LLC owners are receiving compliance letters.

## Step-by-step guide to filling out Form 5472

### Step 1: Gather the necessary information

- **LLC EIN:** Tax identification number.
- **LLC name and address.**
- **Your personal information:** Name, address in your country, tax residence country.
- **Transaction type:** Contribution, withdrawal, loan, payment, etc.
- **Amounts in USD:** Value of each transaction during the tax year.
- **Transfer method:** Wire transfer, ACH, PayPal, etc.

### Step 2: Download the form

Download the latest Form 5472 from IRS.gov. You will also need Form 1120 (pro-forma).

### Step 3: Complete the form parts

Form 5472 has several parts:

| Part | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Part I** | LLC information (name, EIN, address) |
| **Part II** | Foreign owner information (name, address, country) |
| **Part III** | Type of reportable transactions (transaction codes) |
| **Part IV** | Monetary amounts of each transaction |
| **Part V** | Additional information if applicable |

### Step 4: Determine transaction codes

The IRS assigns specific codes for each transaction type:

| Code | Transaction type |
|------|-----------------|
| **01** | Sales of tangible property |
| **02** | Sales of real property |
| **03** | Leases of tangible property |
| **04** | Leases of real property |
| **05** | Interest |
| **06** | Services performed |
| **07** | Services received |
| **08** | Commissions |
| **09** | Royalties |
| **10** | Reimbursements |
| **11** | Insurance |
| **12** | Other amounts (general use) |

For most single-member LLCs, the most common code is **12** (Other amounts), which covers capital contributions and withdrawals.

### Step 5: Prepare the pro-forma Form 1120

The 5472 must be filed **together with Form 1120**. This 1120 is "pro-forma" because the LLC does not pay taxes (it is a disregarded entity), but it serves as a vehicle for the 5472.

Key sections of the 1120:

- **Page 1:** Basic LLC information.
- **Schedule E:** Corporation information.
- **Schedule K:** Other data.
- **5472 attachment:** Attached at the end of the 1120.

### Step 6: Review and submit

Before submitting, verify:

- [ ] All fields are complete.
- [ ] Amounts are in USD.
- [ ] Transaction codes are correct.
- [ ] The 1120 is signed (by an LLC officer, which can be you).
- [ ] The 5472 is signed.
- [ ] You send everything to the correct IRS address.

## Common mistakes

### Mistake 1: Not filing because "the LLC had no income"

Even if the LLC had no income, if you made a capital contribution (deposited money to open the account or pay formation expenses), that is a reportable transaction.

### Mistake 2: Thinking your local accountant will handle it

Many accountants in Latin America are not familiar with Form 5472. Specify that you need a CPA experienced in U.S. taxation to prepare it.

### Mistake 3: Using inconsistent data

The LLC name, EIN, and address must match IRS and state records exactly.

### Mistake 4: Not signing the form

Both the 5472 and 1120 must be signed. An unsigned form is the same as not having filed it.

### Mistake 5: Missing the extension deadline

If you do not file Form 7004 before April 15, you lose the automatic extension and the penalty applies from April 16.

## Practical case

**Situation:** Ana, a graphic designer in Colombia, formed an LLC in Wyoming in 2023. During the year:

1. Made an initial contribution of $5,000 to open the bank account.
2. Transferred $2,000 additional for operating expenses.
3. Withdrew $1,500 for personal expenses.
4. Paid $500 for the Annual Report from her personal account.

**Does she need to file Form 5472?**

Yes. Each of those transactions between Ana (foreign related party) and her LLC (U.S. related party) is reportable.

**What code to use?**

- Code 12 (Other amounts) for all transactions.
- Describe each one in Part IV.

**When?**

Before April 15, 2024 (or October 15 if she requested an extension).

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Form 5472 mean I have to pay taxes in the U.S.?

No. Form 5472 is an **information return**, not an income tax return. You do not pay additional taxes by filing it.

### What if my LLC had no activity?

If there were no transactions between the owner and the LLC (no contributions or withdrawals), you do not need to file Form 5472. However, it is rare for an LLC to have at least one transaction with its owner.

### Can I file Form 5472 myself?

Yes, you can. But the form must be attached to the 1120, which adds complexity. Many choose to hire a CPA.

### Does Form 5472 apply to multi-member LLCs?

Not directly. Multi-member LLCs file Form 1065 and have other obligations. However, similar rules may apply under certain circumstances.

### What if I did not file in previous years?

You should file the delinquent years as soon as possible. The IRS has a "delinquent filing" program that can reduce penalties if you file voluntarily.
