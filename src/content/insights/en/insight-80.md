**Planning international inheritances** is the process of organizing the transfer of your wealth to your heirs when your assets, beneficiaries, or residence span multiple countries. Without proper planning, your heirs may face years of legal proceedings, multiple taxes, and jurisdictional conflicts.

In this guide, we explain **how to plan international inheritances** in 2026: wills, trusts, forced heirship, inheritance taxes, digital assets, and Stripe's role in passing digital businesses.

## 1. Why International Inheritance Planning Matters

### The International Inheritance Challenge

| Problem | Description | Consequence |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| **Multiple jurisdictions** | Assets in 2+ countries, each with its own laws | Parallel probates, duplicated costs |
| **Forced heirship** | Some countries mandate portions for certain heirs | Your will may be invalid |
| **Multiple taxes** | Inheritance tax in each country with assets | Up to 80% of wealth in taxes |
| **Probate** | Public court process, slow, expensive | 6 months to 5 years, assets frozen |
| **Conflict of laws** | Which country applies its law? Residence, nationality, or asset location | Legal uncertainty |
| **Digital assets** | Stripe, crypto, online accounts: no plan = lost | Total loss of the asset |

### Who Needs an International Inheritance Plan?

```
You need an international inheritance plan if:
├── You have assets in 2 or more countries
├── You live in a different country from your nationality
├── Your heirs live in another country
├── You own property abroad
├── You have international investment accounts
├── You hold crypto assets or digital accounts
├── You run an online business (SaaS, e-commerce)
├── You have a US LLC or corporation
└── You have a Stripe account with recurring revenue
```

> In 2026, over 40% of international estates lose at least 30% of their value due to lack of cross-border succession planning.

## 2. International Wills

### Types of Wills

| Type | Description | International Validity |
|------|-------------|----------------------|
| **Holographic will** | Handwritten by the testator | Valid in some countries, not all |
| **Notarial will** | Executed before a notary public | Generally valid in civil law countries |
| **International will** | Washington Convention format (1973) | Valid in 20+ signatory countries |
| **US will** | Before notary + witnesses (varies by state) | Valid in US, may require probate |
| **Switzerland/UK will** | Before witnesses, no notary | Valid in common law, may not be in civil law |

### Can You Use a Single Will?

```
Reality: A single will is rarely sufficient for international assets.

Problems:
- Each country where you have assets may require a separate legal process
- The will must comply with each country's laws
- Forced heirship rules vary by country

Recommended solution:
├── Primary will in your country of residence
├── Separate wills for foreign real estate
├── Trust for international financial assets
└── Letter of instructions for digital assets
```

## 3. Forced Heirship

### Which Countries Have Forced Heirship?

| Country/Region | Forced Heirship | Protected Heirs |
|---------------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Spain** | Yes - 2/3 of estate | Children (2/3: legitime + improvement) |
| **France** | Yes - 50-75% depending on children | Descendants |
| **Italy** | Yes - 50-75% depending on children | Descendants, spouse |
| **Germany** | Yes - 50% of legal portion | Descendants, spouse, parents |
| **Brazil** | Yes - 50% of estate | Descendants, spouse |
| **Mexico** | Yes - 50% of estate | Descendants, spouse |
| **Colombia** | Yes - 50% of estate | Descendants |
| **Argentina** | Yes - 80% of estate | Descendants |
| **Switzerland** | Yes (reduced in 2023) | Descendants, spouse |
| **US** | No (except Louisiana) | Free disposition |
| **UK** | No (but claims possible) | Free disposition |
| **Panama** | Yes - 50% | Descendants |
| **UAE** | No (for non-Muslims) | Free disposition |

### How to Handle Forced Heirship

| Strategy | How It Works | Considerations |
|----------|--------------|----------------|
| **Common law trust** | Trust assets are not part of the estate | Not recognized in all civil law countries |
| **Life insurance** | Beneficiaries receive directly | Bypasses probate, but can be contested |
| **Lifetime gifts** | Transfer assets before death | Gift tax, look-back period |
| **Change of residence** | Applicable law changes with residence | Must actually reside in the country |
| **Choice of applicable law** | EU Regulation 650/2012 (successions) | Can choose nationality law |
| **Corporate structure** | Company (not you) owns assets | Shares are inherited, not assets |

### Example: Resident in Spain with US Assets

```
Situation:
- Residence: Spain (forced heirship: 2/3 for children)
- US assets: Florida property + LLC
- Nationality: Spanish

Problem:
- Spain applies its forced heirship law to your entire global estate
- You cannot freely dispose of your wealth

Solutions:
1. Irrevocable trust in the US (trust assets are not yours)
2. Life insurance for non-forced heirs
3. Lifetime gifts (with limits and timelines)
4. Choice of nationality law if applicable (EU 650/2012)
```

| Aspect | Revocable Trust | Irrevocable Trust |
|--------|----------------|------------------|
| **Avoids probate** | Yes | Yes |
| **Forced heirship protection** | Limited | High |
| **Control during life** | Full | Limited |
| **Taxes** | You pay as individual | Trust pays |
| **Cost** | $1,500-3,000 | $3,000-10,000+ |
| **International recognition** | Limited in civil law | Limited in civil law |

### Trust for International Families

```
International Family Trust:

SETTLOR (you)
  ├── Contribute assets to trust
  └── Define rules (trust deed)

TRUSTEE (professional or family)
  ├── Manages assets
  ├── Distributes to beneficiaries per rules
  └── Can be in Switzerland, Cayman Islands, NZ

BENEFICIARIES (your heirs)
  ├── Children (in Spain, France, etc.)
  ├── Spouse
  └── Other family members

ADVANTAGES:
- Trust assets bypass probate in every country
- Forced heirship doesn't apply (in common law)
- Flexible distribution based on needs
- Total privacy
```

### Double Taxation

```
Problem: Paying inheritance tax in 2+ countries for the same assets.

Solutions:
1. Treaty shopping: Review double tax treaties between countries
2. Corporate structure: Company owns assets, not you directly
3. Trust: Trust assets are not part of your personal estate
4. Foreign tax credit: Pay in one country, credit in another
5. Change tax residence before death

Example:
- Tax residence: UK
- Assets: Properties in US
- No plan: Pay US estate tax + UK inheritance tax
- With plan: Trust structure + corporate holding
```

## 7. Corporate Structure for Succession

### Why Use a Structure

| Advantage | Without Structure | With Structure |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Asset inheritance** | Each asset requires separate succession | You inherit the company, not assets |
| **Probate** | Multiple processes in each country | Single process |
| **Forced heirship** | Applies to all assets | Company is a separate entity |
| **Taxes** | Tax in each country per asset | Single tax on shares |
| **Continuity** | Assets frozen during probate | Business continues operating |
| **Privacy** | Probate is public | Company is not publicized |

### Succession Structure with Holding

```
BEFORE DEATH:

HOLDING (Delaware / Panama)
  ├── Owns IP (code, trademark, patents)
  ├── Owns LLC #1 (Stripe - SaaS)
  ├── Owns LLC #2 (Miami property)
  ├── Owns LLC #3 (Financial investments)
  ├── International bank account
  └── Crypto assets (corporate wallet)

YOU are the sole shareholder of the holding.

AFTER DEATH:

HOLDING → Shares pass to your heirs
  ├── No need to transfer each asset
  ├── Stripe continues operating (LLC owner doesn't change)
  ├── Properties stay in their LLCs
  └── Heirs receive holding shares

PROCESS:
1. Heirs present your will
2. Holding shares are transferred
3. Everything else remains the same
4. Stripe: only update beneficial owner if needed
```

## 10. Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use my US will for assets in Spain?

It depends. For assets in Spain, Spanish law may require forced heirship compliance. A trust can be a better solution.

### Do heirs pay taxes in my country or theirs?

Generally, heirs pay tax where assets are located and in their country of residence. Double tax treaties can help.

### What happens to my Stripe account when I die?

Without a designated successor, Stripe may freeze the account. Designate an Admin successor today in Stripe Dashboard.

### How do my children inherit crypto assets?

They need the seed phrase. Store it in a safe deposit box + password manager with emergency access. Document in your will.

### Is a trust better than a will?

It depends on your situation. Trust: avoids probate, protects against forced heirship, private. Will: simpler, but goes through probate and is public.

### Do I need a lawyer in each country?

Ideally yes, at least for countries where you own real estate or have tax residence. A specialized international succession lawyer can coordinate.
