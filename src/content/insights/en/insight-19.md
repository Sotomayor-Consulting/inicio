Many Latin American entrepreneurs open an LLC in the United States, obtain their EIN, start operating… and never file a tax return. Some believe it is not necessary, others think "the IRS will never find them," and many simply did not know they had obligations.

The question that eventually comes is: **what happens if you never filed taxes in the United States?**

In this article, we explain the real consequences, the risks you face, and the steps to regularize your situation if you are already in this position.

## Does the IRS really find out?

Yes. The IRS has multiple mechanisms to detect taxpayers who have not filed returns:

- **Tax information exchange agreements** with over 100 countries, including most Latin American countries.
- **Bank and fintech reports:** Mercury, Relay, Wise, and other institutions report activity to the IRS through forms like 1099.
- **Payment platform reports:** Stripe, PayPal, and Amazon issue 1099-K when transaction thresholds are exceeded.
- **State registration data:** The Secretary of State has your LLC on record and can share information.
- **Anonymous tips:** The IRS has whistleblower reward programs.

> **Reality:** Ignoring tax obligations does not mean the IRS does not know your LLC exists and is operating.

## Consequences of not filing

### Penalties for not filing informational forms

This is one of the most serious and least known consequences. For a single-member LLC owned by a foreigner, Form 5472 is mandatory. Not filing can result in:

- **$25,000 penalty** for each unfiled form.
- **Additional penalties** if the IRS determines there was intent to evade.
- The penalty applies **for each year** not filed.

### Penalties for not filing the Annual Report

Each state has its own penalties:

| State | Late penalty | Additional consequence |
|-------|-------------|----------------------|
| **Wyoming** | $50–$150 | LLC suspension |
| **Florida** | $400 | Suspension and loss of good standing |
| **Delaware** | $200 + interest | Suspension and additional charges |

### LLC suspension or dissolution

If you do not file the Annual Report for several years, the state can:

- Declare your LLC **inactive**.
- **Administratively dissolve** the company.
- Impose **reinstatement fees** that can exceed $500.

### Problems with banks and payment platforms

When an LLC is suspended:

- **Banks like Mercury or Relay can freeze or close your accounts.**
- **Stripe and PayPal can suspend payment processing.**
- **Amazon FBA can close your seller account.**
- Regaining access can take months.

### Accumulated interest and penalties

If you owed taxes and did not pay, the IRS charges:

- **Interest** on the unpaid amount (calculated daily).
- **Failure-to-file penalty:** 5% of unpaid tax for each month late (max 25%).
- **Failure-to-pay penalty:** 0.5% of unpaid tax for each month late.

### Risk of legal proceedings

In extreme cases of tax evasion, the IRS can:

- Issue a **levy** on bank accounts.
- Initiate **criminal proceedings** (uncommon for foreigners, but possible in fraud cases).

## What if you never opened a bank account for your LLC?

Many believe that if they never activated the LLC (no bank account, no income), they have no obligations. This is partially true, but with nuances:

- If the LLC was formed but never had activity, **it may still have informational obligations** depending on the tax year.
- The **Annual Report must still be filed** even if the LLC has no income, to keep it active.
- If you do not plan to use the LLC, the best option is to **formally dissolve it** to avoid future obligations.

## Steps to regularize your situation

If you are already in this situation, here are the recommended steps:

### Step 1: Determine your current situation

- Is the LLC active or suspended?
- Which years did you not file forms?
- Did you have income in those years?
- Did you open bank accounts or payment processors?

### Step 2: File the pending forms

The IRS allows filing late returns through programs such as:

- **Delinquent Information Return Submission:** To file informational forms (5472, 1120) without automatic penalties if there was no income.
- **Streamlined Filing Procedures:** For taxpayers who did not file but had no intent to evade.

> **Important:** It is not recommended to simply "start filing" without first regularizing previous years, as this can raise red flags.

### Step 3: Regularize the LLC's state status

- If the LLC was suspended, you must pay outstanding penalties and file missing Annual Reports.
- If it was dissolved, you may need to reinstate it or form a new LLC.

### Step 4: Evaluate whether to keep the LLC

If the LLC is not generating benefits that justify its maintenance, it may be better to formally dissolve it.

| Myth | Reality |

| "If I had no income, I do not have to file" | Informational forms may be mandatory without income |
| "I can dissolve the LLC and forget about it" | Tax obligations exist while the LLC was active |
| "They will never find me" | Banks and fintechs report activity to the IRS |

## How long does the IRS have to claim?

The IRS generally has **3 years** to audit a filed return. However:

- If you filed no return, **there is no statute of limitations** — the IRS can claim at any time.
- For tax fraud, the period extends to **6 years**.
- Penalties for not filing informational forms (like Form 5472) **do not expire** as long as the form is not filed.

## Can you go to jail for not filing?

It is unlikely for most cases, but possible in situations of:

- **Intentional tax evasion** (not just omission, but actions to hide income).
- **Tax fraud** with significant amounts.
- **Repeated non-filing** after IRS notifications.

For most Latin American entrepreneurs with LLCs, the real risk is financial penalties, not jail. But ignoring the problem does not make it disappear.
