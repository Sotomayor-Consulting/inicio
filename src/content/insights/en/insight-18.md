One of the most widespread myths among Latin American entrepreneurs is that **opening an LLC in the United States automatically means paying no taxes**. This idea, fueled by incomplete information on social media, has led many to make decisions without understanding the real tax implications.

In this article, we debunk this myth and explain what obligations an LLC really has before the IRS and the states.

## Where does this myth come from?

The confusion arises because, in certain cases, a well-structured LLC can have significant tax advantages. For example:

- A single-member foreign LLC with no physical presence in the U.S. and no U.S.-source income may not owe federal income tax.
- The LLC has "pass-through" taxation, meaning the entity itself does not pay taxes at the corporate level.

However, **not paying corporate taxes is not the same as having no tax obligations**. Many people hear "pass-through" or "disregarded entity" and assume it means "zero taxes," when the reality is much more complex.

## The truth: An LLC is not an automatic tax shield

Having an LLC does not mean you or your company are exempt from taxes. Tax obligations depend on multiple factors:

### Factors that determine if your LLC pays taxes

| Factor | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| **Owner's tax residency** | Determines where and how you report income |
| **Source of income** | Whether income is from U.S. or foreign sources |
| **Physical presence in U.S.** | Office, employees, inventory within the country |
| **Type of activity** | Digital services vs. physical products vs. investments |
| **Number of members** | Single-member vs multi-member has different treatment |
| **Tax elections** | Electing S-Corp or C-Corp changes everything |

## Real LLC taxation scenarios

### Scenario 1: Foreign LLC with no U.S. operations and no U.S.-source income

In this case, the LLC likely **owes no federal income tax**, but **must file informational forms** such as:

- Form 5472 (related party transactions).
- Form 1120 pro forma.
- State Annual Report.

**Conclusion:** No tax payment, but reporting obligations exist. Ignoring them can lead to fines.

### Scenario 2: LLC with U.S.-source income (ECI)

If the LLC sells products or services to U.S. clients or has physical presence in the country, the income may be considered Effectively Connected Income (ECI) and **subject to federal taxes**.

**Conclusion:** Yes, it pays taxes in the U.S. and must file additional returns.

### Scenario 3: Multi-member LLC

A multi-member LLC is taxed as a partnership. It must file Form 1065 and issue Schedule K-1 for each member. Although the LLC does not directly pay taxes, **members report their share of profits** on their personal returns.

**Conclusion:** The LLC does not pay entity-level taxes, but members do declare and pay according to their share.

### Scenario 4: LLC electing S-Corp or C-Corp taxation

If the LLC made a tax election to be taxed as an S-Corp or C-Corp, the rules change completely. In particular, a C-Corp **pays corporate-level taxes**, plus shareholders pay taxes on dividends (double taxation).

**Conclusion:** In this case, the LLC directly pays taxes.

## Obligations that exist even if you owe no taxes

Even in the most favorable scenario (no U.S.-source income and no physical presence), your LLC has obligations:

| Obligation | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Annual Report** | Annual report in the state of registration |
| **Registered Agent** | Must remain active all year |
| **Form 5472** | If single-member LLC owned by a foreigner |
| **Form 1120 pro forma** | Informational return before the IRS |
| **State Franchise Tax** | In states like Delaware or California |
| **Bookkeeping** | Income and expense records |

## The danger of opening an LLC without understanding the obligations

Many entrepreneurs open their LLC, obtain the EIN, and then assume "nothing happens" if they do not file forms or declare. This is a recipe for serious problems:

### Possible consequences

- **IRS fines:** Not filing Form 5472 can result in fines of up to **$25,000 per unfiled form**.
- **LLC suspension:** The state can dissolve your company for not filing the Annual Report.
- **Banking problems:** Banks can close your accounts if the LLC is not in good standing.
- **Inability to reinstate:** Reinstating a suspended LLC can be costly and complicated.
- **Immigration risks:** Although the LLC does not grant residency, tax problems can affect future immigration applications.
- **Unplanned double taxation:** If not structured correctly, you could pay taxes in both the U.S. and your country of residence.

## What "pass-through" and "disregarded entity" really mean

| Term | Means | Does NOT mean |
|------|-------|---------------|
| **Pass-through** | Profits pass to members | That nothing needs to be declared |
| **Disregarded entity** | LLC is ignored as separate entity for tax purposes | That there are no informational obligations |
| **No corporate tax** | LLC does not pay corporate tax | That the owner pays no taxes |

## So, is an LLC useless?

**No, it is very useful.** But you need to understand how to use it correctly.

A well-structured and compliant LLC can offer you:

- Access to the dollar financial system.
- Asset protection.
- International credibility.
- Dollar operations.
- Global scalability.

But all of this works **only if you keep your LLC in compliance**.

## Basic compliance checklist

Before opening an LLC, make sure you understand and budget for these obligations:

- [ ] Annual Report in the state of registration.
- [ ] Active Registered Agent.
- [ ] Form 5472 (if applicable).
- [ ] Form 1120 pro forma (if applicable).
- [ ] Tax return in your country of residence.
- [ ] Separate bank account for the LLC.
- [ ] Income and expense records.
- [ ] Updated Operating Agreement.

## Myths and realities about LLCs and taxes

| Myth | Reality |
|------|---------|
| "An LLC pays no taxes" | Depends on the case; may pay or just file informational forms |
| "If I have no income, I owe nothing" | You may still have informational obligations |
| "The LLC is for not declaring" | The LLC may increase your reporting obligations |
| "I only pay once when opening it" | The LLC has annual maintenance costs |
| "The IRS will never find me" | The IRS has information exchange agreements with many countries |

## Conclusion

Opening an LLC thinking it automatically pays no taxes is one of the most costly mistakes you can make as an entrepreneur. The reality is:

- An LLC **may** not owe federal income tax in certain cases.
- But it **always** has reporting and maintenance obligations.
- Ignoring these obligations can result in fines far exceeding any savings.

The key is not to avoid taxes, but to **structure your LLC correctly** from the start, understanding your obligations and meeting them.
