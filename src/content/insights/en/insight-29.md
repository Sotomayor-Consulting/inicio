Forming an LLC in the United States is relatively simple, but doing it **correctly** is another story. Many Latin American entrepreneurs discover months or years later that their LLC was poorly structured, and the consequences can be serious: problems with the IRS, frozen bank accounts, disputes between partners, and even the loss of limited liability protection.

In this guide, we explain the most common structuring problems, how to detect them, and how to fix them.

## What does "poorly structured LLC" mean?

A poorly structured LLC is one that does not meet one or more of the legal, tax, or operational requirements necessary to function correctly. This can be due to:

| Cause | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Incorrect formation** | Wrong state, wrong entity type, errors in documents |
| **Incomplete documentation** | Missing Operating Agreement, incorrect EIN, inadequate Registered Agent |
| **Wrong tax classification** | Single-member treated as partnership, or vice versa |
| **Ongoing non-compliance** | Annual Reports not filed, 5472 not filed, outdated address |
| **Incorrect partner structure** | Poorly defined percentages, undocumented verbal agreements |
| **Mixing finances** | Unseparated accounts, personal expenses from the LLC |

## Most common problems

### 1. Wrong formation state

**The mistake:** Choosing a state just because it is popular (Delaware) without considering your business's specific needs.

**Why it happens:**
- "Everyone chooses Delaware."
- "Delaware has the best corporate laws."

**Consequences:**
| Aspect | Delaware | Wyoming/Florida |
|---------|----------|----------------|
| **Formation cost** | High ($300-500) | Low ($100-200) |
| **Annual cost** | $300 (franchise tax) | $60-138.75 (Annual Report) |
| **Privacy** | Low (information is public) | High (Wyoming) |
| **Ideal for** | Companies with investors | Freelancers, small agencies |

**Solution:** Evaluate your situation. For most individual entrepreneurs or small agencies, Wyoming or Florida are better options.

### 2. Missing Operating Agreement

**The mistake:** Not drafting an Operating Agreement that defines how the LLC works.

**Why it happens:**
- The formation provider does not include it.
- "It's a single-member LLC, I don't need one."

**Consequences:**
- If you have a partner, there are no clear rules on profit distribution, decision-making, or dispute resolution.
- Banks (Mercury, Relay) may request it and without it, they can deny the account.
- In a legal dispute, the state applies default rules that may not be favorable.

**Solution:** Draft a complete Operating Agreement, even if you are the sole owner. It should include:
- Ownership percentages.
- Profit and loss distribution.
- Voting rights and decision-making.
- Procedure for adding or removing partners.
- LLC dissolution.

### 3. Incorrect EIN or wrong tax classification

**The mistake:** Applying for the EIN with the wrong entity type.

**Why it happens:**
- Lack of knowledge about tax classification options.
- The agent who helped with the EIN did not ask for details.

**Consequences:**
| What you requested | What the IRS recorded | Problem |
|-------------------|----------------------|---------|
| Single-member LLC | Disregarded entity (correct) | No problem |
| Single-member LLC | Partnership (incorrect) | IRS expects Form 1065, not 5472 |
| Multi-member LLC | Disregarded entity (incorrect) | IRS expects 5472, not 1065 |
| LLC | Corporation (incorrect) | LLC must pay corporate tax |

**Solution:** Verify your tax classification with the IRS. If incorrect, request a correction or a new EIN.

### 4. Operating Agreement that does not reflect reality

**The mistake:** Having a generic Operating Agreement that does not reflect how the LLC actually operates.

**Why it happens:**
- It was downloaded from an internet template.
- The situation changed (new partners, different contributions) but the document was not updated.

**Consequences:**
- Profit distributions do not match what was agreed.
- In a dispute, the court applies the Operating Agreement, not the verbal agreement.
- Problems with the IRS if K-1 percentages do not match the Operating Agreement.

**Solution:** Review and update your Operating Agreement at least once a year, or whenever there is a change in membership or structure.

### 5. Mixing personal and business assets

**The mistake:** Using the LLC account for personal purposes, or vice versa.

**Why it happens:**
- Lack of education about limited liability protection.
- Convenience of using a single account.

**Consequences:**
- **You lose the corporate veil** —a judge can ignore the LLC and go after your personal assets in a lawsuit.
- The bank can close the account for violating terms of service.
- The IRS can question the validity of the LLC for tax purposes.

**Solution:**
- Separate bank accounts (personal and business).
- Separate credit cards.
- All invoices and contracts in the LLC's name.
- Document any transfers between accounts as loans or distributions.

### 6. Inadequate Registered Agent

**The mistake:** Using an unreliable Registered Agent, or being your own Registered Agent without a physical address in the state.

**Why it happens:**
- The formation provider included a basic Registered Agent.
- The owner listed their personal address (which is not in the LLC's state).

**Consequences:**
- Not receiving important legal notices (lawsuits, state notifications).
- The LLC can be suspended without you knowing.
- Not receiving the Annual Report renewal notice.

**Solution:** Use a professional Registered Agent service. It should have:
- Physical address in the LLC's state.
- Email notifications.
- Document scanning and forwarding.
- Automatic renewal.

### 7. Poorly defined ownership percentages

**The mistake:** Having ownership percentages that do not reflect the partners' actual contributions.

**Why it happens:**
- "We are 50/50 partners" without considering who contributes more capital or work.
- Initial contributions were not documented.

**Consequences:**
- Disputes between partners.
- Unfair profit distribution.
- Difficulty adding new partners.
- Problems with the IRS if K-1s do not reflect reality.

**Solution:** Clearly define in the Operating Agreement:
- Initial capital contributions.
- Sweat equity contributions.
- Profit and loss distribution.
- Mechanism to adjust percentages in the future.

### 8. Not considering tax treatment in your country of residence

**The mistake:** Forming the LLC without considering how your country of residence will treat it for tax purposes.

**Why it happens:**
- Assuming U.S. tax treatment is the same in all countries.
- Not consulting with a local accountant.

**Consequences:**
- **Hybrid mismatch:** The U.S. sees the LLC as a disregarded entity; your country sees it as a corporation.
- Potential double taxation.
- Unexpected tax obligations in your country.
- Penalties for not filing local returns.

**Solution:** Consult with an accountant in your country of residence BEFORE forming the LLC. Ask:
- How does my country classify a U.S. LLC?
- What returns must I file locally?
- Is there a double taxation risk?
- Should I consider another structure?

### 9. No exit plan

**The mistake:** Not defining what happens if a partner wants to leave, if the LLC is dissolved, or if someone dies.

**Why it happens:**
- "It won't happen." (It always does.)
- Discomfort discussing these topics.

**Consequences:**
- If a partner wants to leave, there are no rules to value their share.
- If a partner dies, their heirs may claim control of the LLC.
- The LLC may have to be forcibly dissolved.

**Solution:** Include in the Operating Agreement:
- Buy-sell clause.
- Valuation method for ownership share.
- Right of first refusal for remaining partners.
- What happens in case of death, disability, or bankruptcy.

### 10. Ignoring the Corporate Transparency Act (BOI)

**The mistake:** Not filing the Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) with FinCEN.

**Why it happens:**
- Lack of awareness of the new law (effective since 2024).
- Assuming the agent who formed the LLC will do it.

**Consequences:**
- Penalty of up to **$10,000**.
- Possible jail time (up to 2 years).
- The LLC can be suspended.

**Solution:** File the BOI within the deadline (90 days for new LLCs, 30 days for changes). If the deadline has passed, file as soon as possible.

## How to detect if your LLC is poorly structured

### Self-assessment

Answer these questions:

| Question | Yes (good) | No (problem) |
|----------|-----------|--------------|
| Do you have a written Operating Agreement? | ✅ | ❌ |
| Does the LLC name match exactly on all documents? | ✅ | ❌ |
| Do you have a separate bank account for the LLC? | ✅ | ❌ |
| Do you use the LLC account only for business expenses? | ✅ | ❌ |
| Do you know which IRS form to file (5472 or 1065)? | ✅ | ❌ |
| Have you filed all required returns on time? | ✅ | ❌ |
| Is your Registered Agent a professional service? | ✅ | ❌ |
| Do your partners have a signed agreement? | ✅ | ❌ |
| Have you filed the BOI with FinCEN? | ✅ | ❌ |
| Do you know how your country treats your LLC for tax purposes? | ✅ | ❌ |
| Is your address updated with the IRS? | ✅ | ❌ |
| Do you have an exit or succession plan? | ✅ | ❌ |

**If you answered "No" to 3 or more questions, your LLC likely needs restructuring.**

## How to fix a poorly structured LLC

### Simple fixes

| Problem | Solution | Cost |
|---------|----------|------|
| Missing Operating Agreement | Draft it and have all partners sign | $200 - $500 |
| Incorrect EIN | Request correction or new EIN | $0 (directly with IRS) |
| Inadequate Registered Agent | Switch to a professional service | $50 - $150/year |
| Outdated address | File Form 8822-B | $0 |
| Wrong tax classification | Request correction from IRS | $0 - $500 (with CPA) |

### Complex fixes

| Problem | Solution | Cost | Time |
|---------|----------|------|------|
| Wrong state | Convert or migrate LLC to another state | $500 - $2,000 | 2-4 weeks |
| Partner restructuring | New Operating Agreement + new tax structure | $1,000 - $3,000 | 2-4 weeks |
| International hybrid mismatch | Tax restructuring with accountants in both countries | $2,000 - $5,000 | 4-8 weeks |
| Dissolved LLC for non-compliance | Reinstatement + regularization | $500 - $3,000 | 2-6 weeks |
| Tax classification change | File Form 8832 or 2553 | $500 - $1,500 | 4-8 weeks |

### When to close and start over

Sometimes it is cheaper and faster to close the poorly structured LLC and form a new one correctly.

**Consider closing and starting over if:**
- The LLC has been dissolved for more than 3 years.
- Correction costs are higher than a new LLC.
- The LLC has legal problems or debts.
- You need a clean EIN (no non-compliance history).
- You changed countries or need another state.

**Consider restructuring if:**
- The LLC has active contracts and recurring clients.
- The LLC has tax history with the IRS.
- The LLC has an active bank account.
- Correction costs are lower than starting over.

## Practical cases

### Case 1: Delaware for a freelancer

**Situation:** Sofia, a graphic designer in Argentina, formed her LLC in Delaware because "everyone was doing it." She paid $300 per year in franchise tax. She never needed investors.

**Problem:**
- Delaware was unnecessarily expensive for her single-member business.
- She could have saved $240/year in Wyoming or $161.25/year in Florida.

**Solution:**
- Evaluated migrating the LLC to Wyoming.
- Migration cost: $500.
- Annual savings: $240.
- Recouped investment in just over 2 years.

### Case 2: Generic Operating Agreement

**Situation:** Miguel and Ana are 50% partners in an agency. Miguel contributed the initial capital; Ana contributes the work. They have no Operating Agreement.

**Problem:**
- If they separate, there are no clear rules on who gets what.
- The bank requested the Operating Agreement and they did not have one.
- They cannot open a Relay account.

**Solution:**
- Drafted an Operating Agreement reflecting:
  - Miguel: 50% (capital contribution).
  - Ana: 50% (sweat equity).
  - Profit distribution: 50/50 after operating expenses.
  - Dispute resolution mechanism.
- Cost: $400.
- Relay approved the account.

### Case 3: Wrong tax classification

**Situation:** Carlos has a single-member LLC in Florida. When applying for the EIN, he selected "Partnership" by mistake. The IRS expects a Form 1065 each year, but Carlos never filed it because he did not know.

**Problem:**
- The IRS has the LLC registered as a partnership.
- Carlos owes $220/month in penalties since forming the LLC.
- He has not filed returns for 3 years.

**Solution:**
- Requested a new EIN with the correct classification (disregarded entity).
- Cost: $0 (directly with the IRS).
- Filed late 5472s under delinquent filing.
- Paid reduced penalties.
- Total cost: $1,200 (CPA) + reduced penalties.

## How to structure your LLC correctly from the start

### Correct structuring checklist

- [ ] Choose the right state for your business (Wyoming/Florida for most).
- [ ] Hire a professional Registered Agent.
- [ ] Draft a complete Operating Agreement.
- [ ] Apply for the EIN with the correct tax classification.
- [ ] Open a separate business bank account.
- [ ] Set up accounting from day one.
- [ ] Consult with a local accountant about tax treatment in your country.
- [ ] File the BOI with FinCEN within the deadline.
- [ ] Set up an annual compliance calendar.
- [ ] Define an exit or succession plan.

## Conclusion

A poorly structured LLC can cost you money, time, and peace of mind. The most common problems —wrong state, missing Operating Agreement, incorrect tax classification— are preventable with good initial advice.

If you already have an LLC and suspect it is poorly structured, **do not wait**. Do a self-assessment, identify the problems, and fix them. In most cases, corrections are possible and the cost is less than the consequences of doing nothing.
