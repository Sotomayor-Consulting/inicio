E-commerce is one of the sectors that benefits most from an LLC in the United States. Platforms like Amazon FBA, Shopify, Etsy, and eBay are designed to operate with U.S. entities, and having an LLC can make the difference between a business with limitations and one that scales globally.

In this article, we explain step by step how to use your LLC for e-commerce, which platforms you can leverage, and what to consider to operate correctly.

## Why is an LLC key for e-commerce?

Selling online from Latin America using a U.S. LLC offers advantages you simply would not have as an individual:

- **Access to Stripe, Shopify Payments, and Amazon Pay** without geographical restrictions.
- **Dollar bank accounts** to receive payments directly.
- **Credibility with clients and suppliers** by operating as a U.S. company.
- **Asset protection** by separating your personal finances from the business.
- **Scalability** to hire employees, open wholesale accounts, and expand to new channels.

## Step 1: Choose the right state for e-commerce

For e-commerce businesses, the most recommended states are:

- **Wyoming:** No state tax, privacy, low cost -- $60
- **Florida:** No state personal income tax, LATAM proximity -- $138.75
- **Delaware:** Ideal if seeking external investment -- $300
**Recommendation:** For most e-commerce businesses operating 100% online with no physical inventory in the U.S., Wyoming offers the best balance of cost, privacy, and simplicity.

> **Note:** If you plan to store inventory in the U.S. (Amazon FBA), you may need to register as a foreign LLC in the state where the warehouse is located, which can create additional tax obligations.

## Step 2: Form the LLC and obtain the EIN

Once you have chosen the state, you must:

1. **File the Articles of Organization** with the Secretary of State.
2. **Appoint a registered agent**.
3. **Obtain the EIN** from the IRS (required for payment platforms and banks).
4. **Draft the Operating Agreement**.

> **Important:** As a non-resident alien, this entire process can be done remotely.

## Step 3: Open bank accounts and payment platforms

### Recommended banks for e-commerce

- **Mercury:** Ideal for digital businesses, integrates with Stripe and Shopify.
- **Relay:** Allows separate accounts for taxes and expenses.
- **Wise Business:** Perfect for receiving international payments and paying suppliers.
- **Novo:** Integrates with accounting tools like QuickBooks and Xero.

### Payment platforms

- **Stripe:** Accepts cards, ideal for custom Shopify stores
- **PayPal Business:** Essential for eBay and international marketplaces
- **Shopify Payments:** Reduced rates, native Shopify integration
- **Amazon Pay:** Ideal if you already sell on Amazon
## Step 4: Set up your online store with the LLC

### Shopify

- Register your store using your LLC name.
- Set up Shopify Payments (requires EIN and business bank account).
- Use your LLC address for terms and conditions.

### Amazon FBA

- Create a professional seller account with your LLC information.
- Amazon prioritizes business accounts over personal accounts.
- You will need EIN and bank account to receive payments.

### Etsy

- You can register your shop as a business.
- Use your EIN for tax purposes.
- Offers integration with international payment processors.

### WooCommerce / Own store

- Use Stripe as your payment processor.
- Set up your LLC as the legal entity on invoices.
- Get a merchant account if volume justifies it.

## Step 5: Tax management for e-commerce

E-commerce has specific tax implications you should know:

### Sales Tax

If your LLC has **nexus** in any state (inventory, employees, office), you may be required to collect and remit sales tax. This applies especially if you use Amazon FBA and Amazon stores your inventory in multiple states.

### Income Tax

- If your customers are outside the U.S. and you have no physical presence, you likely do not owe federal income tax.
- If you sell to U.S. customers, you may have U.S.-source income (ECI) and be subject to taxes.
- Consult with an accountant specializing in international e-commerce.

### Informational forms

Even if you owe no taxes, your LLC likely must file:

- **Form 5472** (foreign single-member LLC).
- **Form 1120 pro forma**.
- **Annual Report** in the state of registration.

## Step 6: Logistics and suppliers

With an LLC you can access:

- **Wholesale accounts** with international suppliers.
- **Amazon FBA:** Send your inventory to Amazon's distribution centers.
- **3PL (Third Party Logistics):** Companies that store and ship your products.
- **Dropshipping:** Work with suppliers who ship directly to the end customer.

### Popular suppliers for e-commerce with LLC

- **Manufacturing:** Alibaba, AliExpress, local suppliers
- **Fulfillment:** Amazon FBA, ShipBob, ShipMonk
- **Dropshipping:** Spocket, Oberlo, CJ Dropshipping
- **Print-on-demand:** Printful, Printify, Gooten
## E-commerce models that work with LLC

**Amazon FBA:** Send your inventory to Amazon, they store, pack, and ship. Ideal for physical products.

**Shopify + Dropshipping:** Set up your store and suppliers ship directly to the customer. Low risk, low initial cost.

**Etsy + Digital products:** Sell designs, templates, downloadable resources. No physical inventory, high profitability.

**Own store + Stripe:** Full control over your brand and customer experience.

**eBay + Used/vintage products:** Global marketplace with millions of active buyers.

## Common mistakes in e-commerce with LLC

### Not registering for Sales Tax

If you store inventory in a state (especially with Amazon FBA), you may have sales tax obligations to fulfill.

### Mixing personal and business inventory

Your LLC must purchase the inventory, not you as an individual. This is key for asset protection and accounting clarity.

### Ignoring import/export regulations

If you import products from China or other countries to sell in the U.S., you must comply with customs and labeling regulations.

### Using personal accounts on payment platforms

Stripe, PayPal, and other platforms may suspend personal accounts with high volume. Always use business accounts linked to your LLC.

Using an LLC for e-commerce in the United States is a strategy that removes geographical, financial, and credibility barriers.
