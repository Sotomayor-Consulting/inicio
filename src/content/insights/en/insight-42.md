International transfers are an essential part of global business, but they are also one of the financial movements that receive the most attention from tax authorities. Knowing **how to justify international transfers** correctly is key to avoiding IRS inquiries, money laundering fines, and problems with your bank.

Below, we explain everything you need to properly document and justify each international transfer you make or receive.

## 1. Why Are International Transfers Scrutinized?

Tax and financial authorities pay special attention to international transfers because:

### Reasons for scrutiny:
- **Anti-Money Laundering (AML)**: International transfers can be used to launder money
- **Tax evasion**: The IRS looks for undeclared income hidden in foreign accounts
- **Illicit financing**: Transfers to certain countries may be linked to illegal activities
- **Currency controls**: Some countries have exchange restrictions and monitor capital outflows

### Consequences of not justifying:
- Bank freezing of funds
- Report to FinCEN (Financial Crimes Enforcement Network)
- IRS audit
- Significant penalties
- Potential criminal charges

## 2. Essential Documentation for Each Transfer

For each international transfer you make, you must maintain:

### Basic documents:
- **Transfer receipt** (SWIFT MT103, bank confirmation)
- **Invoice or contract** supporting the underlying transaction
- **Party identification**: Name, address, country, tax ID/EIN
- **Transfer purpose**: Clear and detailed description
- **Exchange rate applied** (if currency conversion applies)

### Information the documentation should contain:
- **Transfer date:** Day it was executed
- **Amount:** In original currency and in USD
- **Sender:** Legal name, country, bank account
- **Recipient:** Legal name, country, bank account
- **Concept:** Detailed payment description
- **Supporting document:** Invoice, contract, agreement
## 3. Transfers for Professional Services

If you receive or send money for professional services, you must justify:

### For the recipient:
- **Service agreement** signed by both parties
- **Professional invoice** with detailed service description
- **Deliverables**: Reports, designs, code, consultations
- **Correspondence**: Emails, messages, meetings (agenda, notes)
- **Tax payment receipt** in your country of residence (if applicable)

### For the sender:
- **Form W-8BEN** from the provider (if foreign) or **W-9** (if U.S.)
- **Withholding tax receipt** (if applicable)
- **Company payment policy** for vendors

### Practical example:
> A graphic designer in Colombia receives $5,000 USD from a U.S. client. They should have: service agreement, professional invoice, design deliverables, emails with specifications, and SWIFT transfer receipt.

## 4. Transfers for Product Sales (E-commerce)

For transfers related to product sales:

### Required documentation:
- **Commercial Invoice** with Incoterms
- **Packing List**
- **Transport document**: Bill of Lading (ocean), Air Waybill (air)
- **Customs Declaration**
- **International purchase agreement**
- **Proof of import duty and tax payment**

### Digital platforms (Shopify, Amazon, etc.):
- Platform statements showing sales
- Platform settlement reports
- Customer invoices (if applicable)

## 5. Transfers Between Own Accounts (Owner to LLC)

If you own a U.S. LLC and transfer funds from your personal foreign account:

### Required documentation:
- **Capital Contribution Agreement** or similar document
- **LLC Operating Agreement** (authorizing capital contributions)
- **Personal bank statement** showing the debit
- **LLC bank statement** showing the credit
- **Accounting record** of the capital contribution

### Important:
- Capital transfers are not taxable income for the LLC
- They must be recorded as "Member Contributions" or "Capital Contributions" in the books

### For distributions (from LLC to your personal account):
- **Distribution resolution** approved by members
- **K-1** (if the LLC is a partnership)
- **Owner's draw record**

## 6. Transfers for Investments

If you make international transfers for investments:

### For real estate investments:
- **Purchase Agreement**
- **Deed**
- **Escrow deposit receipt**
- **Closing Statement (HUD-1)**
- **Transfer tax payment receipt**

### For stock or fund investments:
- **Transaction confirmation** from the broker
- **Investment account opening agreement**
- **Periodic statements** showing the transaction

### For cryptocurrency investments:
- **Transaction record** (hash, date, pair, amount)
- **Deposit receipt** to the exchange
- **Source and destination wallet addresses**
- **USD value** at the time of the transaction

## 7. Family Transfers (Gifts and Support)

Family transfers require special documentation:

### For gifts:
- **Gift letter** signed by both donor and recipient
  - Gift date
  - Amount
  - Description "this is a gift with no expectation of repayment"
  - Both parties' signatures
- **Transfer receipt**
- **Form 709** (if the donor is U.S. and the amount exceeds $18,000 annually in 2025)

### For family support:
- **Affidavit** explaining the nature of the support
- **Proof of family relationship** (birth certificate, marriage certificate)
- **Proof of need** (if applicable, such as medical or educational expenses)

> **Important:** Gifts received from nonresidents are not taxable income for the recipient in the U.S.

## 8. How to Respond to Bank Inquiries

If your bank requests information about an international transfer:

### Steps to follow:
1. **Don't panic**: This is standard AML/KYC compliance procedure
2. **Respond quickly**: Banks have deadlines for receiving documentation
3. **Provide complete documentation**: The more detailed, the better
4. **Keep a record** of all communication with the bank

### Documents the bank may request:
- Source of Funds (SOF)
- Transfer purpose
- Relationship between the parties
- Supporting contract or invoice
- Party identification (passport, license, EIN)

### If the bank freezes your funds:
- Request a written explanation of the freeze reason
- Provide the requested documentation as soon as possible
- Consult a lawyer if the freeze is prolonged

## 10. Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

### Frequent mistakes when justifying transfers:

1. **Generic or vague concepts**: "Payment", "Transfer", "Services" — be specific
2. **Lack of contract**: No written agreement supporting the transaction
3. **Incomplete documentation**: Missing signatures, dates, or identifications
4. **Failing to keep records**: Deleting emails or invoices after the transaction
5. **Structuring**: Multiple small transfers to avoid scrutiny
6. **Mixing personal and business funds**: Makes it difficult to identify each transfer's purpose
7. **Not updating documentation**: Outdated contracts or incorrect addresses

### Best practices:
- **Be transparent**: Always declare the real purpose of the transfer
- **Document in real time**: Don't wait until you are asked to gather papers
- **Maintain consistency**: The concept on the transfer must match the invoice and contract
- **Consult a professional**: If you are unsure how to document a complex transfer
