The IRS is not an agency you want to have problems with. For foreign owners of U.S. LLCs, a tax mistake can mean thousands of dollars in penalties, frozen bank accounts, and even the loss of your LLC.

However, most mistakes are avoidable. In this guide, we compile the most common mistakes international entrepreneurs make with their LLCs and how to avoid them.

## The 10 most common mistakes

### Mistake 1: Not filing Form 5472

**The problem:** Form 5472 is mandatory for every single-member LLC with a foreign owner. The penalty for not filing is **$25,000 per year**.

**Why it happens:**
- The owner does not know this form exists.
- The local accountant is not familiar with it.
- They assume "since I had no income, I don't need to file anything."

**How to avoid it:**
- Schedule Form 5472 on your annual calendar (due April 15).
- Even if the LLC had no income, if there were transactions between the owner and the LLC (capital contribution, withdrawals, expense payments), you must file.
- Work with a CPA who knows foreign-owned LLCs.

### Mistake 2: Not filing the state Annual Report

**The problem:** If you do not file the Annual Report, your LLC can be suspended by the state. This has serious consequences:
- The bank (Mercury, Relay) can freeze your account.
- You lose limited liability protection.
- Clients may stop paying you.

**Why it happens:**
- The owner does not know this annual obligation exists.
- They do not remember the due date.
- They change their address and do not receive state notices.

**How to avoid it:**
- **Wyoming:** January 1 -- $60 + penalty
- **Florida:** May 1 -- $400
- **Delaware:** March 1 -- $200 + penalty
- **New Mexico:** Not required -- N/A
- Set calendar reminders.
- Use a Registered Agent service that notifies you.
- If you change your address, update it with the state and the IRS.

### Mistake 3: Mixing personal and business finances

**The problem:** Using the LLC account for personal expenses violates bank terms and puts limited liability protection at risk.

**Why it happens:**
- It is easier to use one account.
- "It's my money, why can't I use it however I want?"
- No personal account available in the U.S.

**How to avoid it:**
- **Never** use the LLC account for personal expenses.
- Open a personal account with Wise, Payoneer, or a local bank.
- If you need to withdraw money from the LLC, do it as an "Owner's Draw" and document the transaction.
- Use a business credit card for all LLC expenses.

### Mistake 4: Not keeping your address updated with the IRS

**The problem:** The IRS sends all communications to the address registered in the Business Master File. If you change your address and do not update it, you will miss important notices, including:
- Audit notifications.
- Compliance letters.
- Penalty notices.

**Why it happens:**
- The owner moves and forgets to update.
- They do not know they must file Form 8822-B.
- They assume email is sufficient.

**How to avoid it:**
- Every time you change your address, file **Form 8822-B** with the IRS.
- Verify your registered address at least once a year.
- Use a stable address, such as your Registered Agent's.

### Mistake 5: Not understanding the difference between disregarded entity and partnership

**The problem:** Single-member LLC owners who add a partner without updating their tax structure, or multi-member LLC owners who file the wrong form.

**Why it happens:**
- Lack of knowledge about IRS rules.
- Not consulting a professional before changing the structure.

**How to avoid it:**
- **Single-member LLC (foreign owner):** File Form 5472.
- **Multi-member LLC:** File Form 1065.
- If you add a partner, obtain a new EIN and update your structure.
- Consult with a CPA before making membership changes.

### Mistake 6: Not responding to IRS letters

**The problem:** The IRS sends letters for various reasons: information requests, discrepancy notices, penalty notifications. Ignoring them does not make them go away; it makes things worse.

**Why it happens:**
- The letter is in English and the owner does not understand it.
- It looks like spam or a scam.
- Fear of opening it.

**How to avoid it:**
- **Every IRS communication is important.** Open and read it.
- If you do not understand English, translate it with Google Translate or a professional.
- Respond within the given timeframe (usually 30 days).
- If you need help, contact a CPA or enrolled agent.

### Mistake 7: Not getting the correct EIN

**The problem:** When applying for an EIN, some foreign owners make mistakes that are later difficult to correct.

**Common mistakes:**
- Selecting the wrong entity type.
- Using a name that does not exactly match the Certificate of Formation.
- Providing an incorrect address.

**How to avoid it:**
- Verify that the name on the SS-4 matches **exactly** the Certificate of Formation.
- Select "Limited Liability Company" as the entity type.
- Use the same address you registered with the state.
- If you make a mistake, request a correction from the IRS immediately.

### Mistake 8: Not keeping accounting records

**The problem:** The IRS can audit your LLC even if it does not generate income in the U.S. Without proper accounting records, you will not be able to defend your tax position.

**Why it happens:**
- "My LLC is small, I don't need accounting."
- Expenses are paid from the personal account.
- No invoices or contracts.

**How to avoid it:**
- Keep accounting from day one (QuickBooks, Xero, or a spreadsheet).
- Save all invoices and receipts for LLC expenses.
- Document all transactions between the owner and the LLC.
- Keep records for at least 7 years.

### Mistake 9: Ignoring the Corporate Transparency Act (BOI)

**The problem:** Since 2024, most LLCs must report their beneficial ownership information (BOI) to FinCEN. Failure to do so can result in penalties of up to $10,000 and jail time.

**Why it happens:**
- The owner does not know this requirement exists.
- They assume it does not apply to their LLC.
- They do not receive the notification.

**How to avoid it:**
- Verify if your LLC must file the BOI (most small LLCs do).
- File the report with FinCEN within the deadline (90 days for new LLCs, 30 days for changes).
- Consult our BOI guide for more details.

### Mistake 10: Not having a backup banking plan

**The problem:** If Mercury or Relay close your account (for any reason), not having an alternative bank account can stop your business.

**Why it happens:**
- Dependence on a single bank.
- Overconfidence that "it will never happen to me."

**How to avoid it:**
- Have at least 2 active bank accounts (e.g., Mercury and Wise Business, or Relay and Mercury).
- Maintain a balance in each account.
- Have documentation ready to open a new account quickly.

## Mistakes by LLC stage

### Formation stage

- **Choosing the wrong state:** Higher cost, worse protection -- Wyoming or Florida for most cases
- **Not drafting an Operating Agreement:** Internal disputes, bank problems -- Do it before requesting the EIN
- **Unreliable Registered Agent:** Missing important notices -- Use a professional service
- **EIN with incorrect data:** Bank and filing problems -- Verify everything before submitting SS-4
### Operating stage

- **Not separating accounts:** Legal and banking risk -- Always use separate accounts
- **Not filing 5472:** $25,000 penalty -- Schedule it and file every year
- **Personal expenses on the LLC:** Account closure -- Use business card
- **Not documenting transactions:** Audit problems -- Save invoices and contracts
### Compliance stage

- **Not filing Annual Report:** LLC suspension -- Set reminders
- **Ignoring IRS letters:** Penalties and sanctions -- Always open and respond
- **Not updating address:** Missing notices -- Use Form 8822-B
- **Not filing BOI:** $10,000 penalty -- Verify obligation and file
## The cost of mistakes

- **Not filing 5472:** $25,000 per year
- **Not filing Annual Report:** $60 - $400 + reinstatement
- **Not filing BOI:** $10,000 + possible jail
- **Frozen bank account:** Lost income, months of delay
- **Suspended LLC:** Cannot operate, clients leave
- **IRS audit:** Legal costs, penalties, back taxes
## Practical case: What NOT to do

**Situation:** Pablo, an SEO specialist from Peru, formed his LLC in Wyoming in 2022. Here is what he did wrong:

### Year 1 (2022)
- Formed the LLC correctly.
- Obtained the EIN.
- Opened a Mercury account.
- Did not draft an Operating Agreement.
- Did not know Form 5472 existed.

### Year 2 (2023)
- Used the Mercury account for personal expenses (Amazon, Netflix, etc.).
- Did not file Form 5472 (potential penalty: $25,000).
- Moved from Peru to Mexico without updating his address.
- Added a partner without changing his tax structure.
- Did not file Wyoming's Annual Report.
- Ignored an IRS letter about the 5472.

### Result (2024)
- LLC suspended for missing Annual Report.
- Mercury froze his account.
- IRS imposed a $25,000 penalty for not filing 5472.
- Additional penalty for not responding to the IRS letter.
- The partner cannot report income because there is no K-1.
- Pablo lost 3 months of income while resolving.

### Total cost of mistakes
- IRS penalty: $25,000.
- LLC reinstatement: $500.
- Late Annual Report penalty: $60.
- Lost income: $15,000 (3 months without operating).
- Legal fees: $3,000.

**Total: ~$43,560**

All of this could have been avoided with basic compliance.

## Checklist: Mistakes to avoid

- [ ] File Form 5472 every year (if applicable).
- [ ] File the state Annual Report on time.
- [ ] Do not mix personal expenses with the LLC.
- [ ] Keep your address updated with the IRS.
- [ ] Respond to all IRS letters.
- [ ] Have the correct EIN from the start.
- [ ] Keep complete accounting records.
- [ ] File the BOI with FinCEN.
- [ ] Have at least 2 bank accounts.
- [ ] Consult with a CPA before structural changes.
- [ ] Do not add partners without updating the tax structure.
- [ ] Read and understand bank emails.

## Conclusion

Mistakes with the IRS are costly, but almost all are avoidable. Most occur due to lack of knowledge, not bad intent.

The key to avoiding problems is:
1. **Know your obligations** (5472, Annual Report, BOI, etc.).
2. **Maintain a compliance calendar** with all important dates.
3. **Respond to all communications** from the IRS and the bank.
4. **Have a team of professionals** (CPA, Registered Agent, legal advisor).
5. **Do not take shortcuts** in tax compliance.
