**Stripe** is one of the most powerful payment platforms in the world, processing payments for millions of businesses in over 40 countries. However, the vast majority of Latin American countries are **not on Stripe's supported countries list** for creating accounts directly.

This means Latin American entrepreneurs need alternative structures to access Stripe. In this guide, we explain **how to open Stripe from Latin America** legally, safely, and efficiently.

## 1. Why Isn't Stripe Available in Latin America?

Stripe operates under specific banking and payment licenses in each country. Expanding to new countries requires:

- **Agreements with local banks** and payment processors
- **Regulatory compliance** with each country's financial laws
- **Risk assessment** of the local market
- **Customer support** in the local language

## 2. Structures to Access Stripe

### Option 1: US Company + Stripe

The most common and recommended approach is incorporating a company in the United States and using that company to create a Stripe account.

#### Steps:

1. **Form an LLC in the US**
   - Choose a state (Delaware, Wyoming, Florida, Texas)
   - Hire a registered agent
   - Obtain an EIN (Employer Identification Number) from the IRS

2. **Open a US business bank account**
   - Traditional banks: Chase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo
   - Digital banks: Mercury, Relay, Novo

3. **Create a Stripe account**
   - Use your US LLC details
   - Link your business bank account
   - Complete identity verification

4. **Process payments**
   - Integrate Stripe into your website or app
   - Receive payments in USD
   - Transfer funds to your US bank account

#### Estimated Costs

| Item | Cost |
|------|------|
| **LLC Registration** | $100 - $500 (state fees) |
| **Registered Agent** | $100 - $300/year |
| **EIN** | Free (IRS) |
| **Bank account** | Free (Mercury, Relay) |
| **Stripe fee** | 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction |
| **Annual maintenance** | $300 - $1,000/year |

#### Advantages
- ✅ Legal and established solution
- ✅ Access to the entire Stripe ecosystem
- ✅ Ability to expand to other services (Atlas, Connect, Capital)

#### Disadvantages
- ❌ Annual maintenance costs
- ❌ US tax obligations (Annual Report, BOI, tax return)
- ❌ Administrative complexity

### Option 2: Stripe Partners (Stripe Connect)

**Stripe Connect** allows platforms to create Stripe accounts for their end users. Some Latin American platforms act as intermediaries.

#### How it works:

```
LATAM Entrepreneur
    ↓
Partner Platform (e.g., DLocal, Payoneer)
    ↓
Stripe (processing)
    ↓
End customer
```

#### Advantages
- ✅ Less bureaucracy
- ✅ Can accept payments faster

#### Disadvantages
- ❌ Higher fees
- ❌ Less control over the account

### Option 3: Stripe Atlas

**Stripe Atlas** is a Stripe service that helps entrepreneurs incorporate a US company (Delaware C-Corp) and open a Stripe account.

#### Ideal for:
- Startups planning to raise capital
- Companies aiming to operate globally
- Those who prefer an integrated solution

#### Cost: $500 (one-time fee)

#### Includes:
- Delaware C-Corp incorporation
- EIN (IRS)
- Bank account (via partnership)
- Stripe account
- Basic legal documentation

#### Limitations:
- C-Corp only (not LLC)
- Higher maintenance costs than LLC
- Ideal for startups, not for all cases

## 3. Required Documents

### To create an LLC in the US:

- **Valid passport**
- **Proof of address** (utility bill, etc.)
- **EIN** (obtained from the IRS)
- **US address** (registered agent + business address)

### To create a Stripe account:

- **Company details** (LLC, EIN, address)
- **US business bank account**
- **Website or app** (for verification)
- **ID document** of the owner

## 4. Taxes and Obligations

### In the US

| Obligation | Frequency | Description |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Annual Report** | Annual | Yearly report to the state |
| **BOI (FinCEN)** | Once + changes | Beneficial ownership report |
| **Federal Tax Return** | Annual | Federal tax filing (Form 1120 or 1040 Schedule C) |
| **State Tax Return** | Annual | State tax filing (when applicable) |

### In Your Home Country

- **Foreign asset declaration:** The LLC must be declared as a foreign controlled company
- **Profit taxation:** Profits distributed by the LLC may be taxed in your country of residence
- **Transfer pricing:** If you have operations between the LLC and companies in your country, documentation is required

> **Important:** Legislation on foreign company taxation varies significantly across Latin American countries. Consult a specialized international tax accountant in your country.

## 7. Risks and Precautions

### What to AVOID

- ❌ **Using third-party Stripe accounts** — Stripe may block the account and hold funds
- ❌ **Lying about your location** — Stripe verifies IP address, documents, and bank data
- ❌ **Creating accounts in countries where you have no real presence** — violates terms of service
- ❌ **Mixing personal and business accounts** — complicates accounting and increases tax risks
- ❌ **Ignoring tax obligations** — both in the US and your home country

### Does Stripe Detect Location Fraud?

Yes. Stripe uses:

- **IP verification** — detects if you're accessing from an unsupported country
- **Identity documents** — validates they are from the country you claim
- **Bank account** — verifies the bank account is from the declared country
- **Address** — confirms the provided address is valid

> Attempting to bypass these systems can result in permanent account suspension and fund holds of up to 180 days.

## 8. Recommended Step-by-Step

### For Individual Entrepreneurs and Freelancers

1. **Form an LLC** in Wyoming or New Mexico (low costs, privacy)
2. **Get an EIN** for free from the IRS
3. **Open a Mercury or Relay account**
4. **Create your Stripe account** with the LLC details
5. **Declare the company** in your country of residence
6. **Keep your accounting up to date**

### For Startups

1. **Use Stripe Atlas** ($500) to form a Delaware C-Corp
2. **Open a bank account** via Mercury or Brex
3. **Set up Stripe** integrated with Atlas
4. **Prepare documentation** for investors
5. **Meet compliance obligations** in the US and your country

### For Multi-Country Operations

1. **Form an international holding company** (Canada, US, or Panama)
2. **Open subsidiaries** in the countries where you operate
3. **Use Stripe Connect** to process payments in multiple currencies
4. **Structure transfer pricing** properly
5. **Centralize compliance management**
