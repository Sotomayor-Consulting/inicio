Protecting the assets you have built with your business is as important as growing them. For international entrepreneurs, U.S. structures �particularly LLCs� offer powerful asset protection tools not always available in their home countries.

In this guide, we explore how to use LLCs and other international structures to protect your assets legally and effectively.

## Why do you need asset protection?

### Common risks for international entrepreneurs

- **Client lawsuits:** An unsatisfied client can sue your business
- **Partner disputes:** Disagreements that end in litigation
- **Suppliers or creditors:** Business debts claiming your personal assets
- **Tax problems:** The IRS or your country's tax authority can levy accounts
- **Divorce or inheritance:** Claims on your assets from personal changes
- **Political/economic instability:** Devaluation, capital controls, expropriation in your country
- **Professional errors:** Negligence or malpractice lawsuits
### The basic principle: asset separation

Asset protection is based on a simple principle: **separate your personal assets from your business risks**. If your business is sued or goes bankrupt, your personal assets �home, savings, investments� are protected.

## The LLC as an asset protection tool

### 1. Limited liability protection

The main benefit of an LLC is that it **separates your personal assets from the company's**. If someone sues the LLC, they can only claim the LLC's assets, not your personal assets.

**What it protects:**
- Your home or personal property.
- Your personal bank accounts.
- Your personal investments.
- Your personal vehicle.

**What it does NOT protect:**
- Assets you mix with the LLC (if you use the LLC account for personal expenses, a judge can "pierce the veil").
- Personal debts secured by your assets.
- Fraud or illegal activity.
- Non-compliance with tax obligations.

### 2. Protection against creditors in your country

Depending on your country of residence, assets inside a U.S. LLC can be harder for local creditors to reach.

**Why?**
- The LLC is registered in the U.S., not in your country.
- A local creditor would have to litigate in the U.S. to reach the assets.
- The cost and complexity of an international lawsuit deter many creditors.

> **Important:** This is not absolute protection. A determined creditor can litigate in the U.S. and win. But it raises the barrier to entry significantly.

### 3. Protection in favorable states (Wyoming, Nevada, Delaware)

Some U.S. states have specific laws protecting assets inside LLCs:

- **Wyoming:** Charge order protection -- A creditor can only receive LLC distributions, not control assets
- **Nevada:** Charge order protection -- Similar to Wyoming
- **Delaware:** Series LLC -- Each series has independent protection
- **Florida:** Homestead exemption -- Protection of primary residence
**Charge order protection:** This is the legal mechanism that limits what a creditor can do with your LLC. If a creditor wins a lawsuit against you personally, they cannot take your LLC's assets. They can only obtain a "charge order" giving them the right to receive any distributions the LLC makes to you �but they cannot force the LLC to make distributions.

### 4. Series LLC: protection by business line

A **Series LLC** allows you to create separate "series" within one LLC. Each series is like an independent LLC for asset protection purposes.

**Example:**
- **Series A:** Digital marketing agency.
- **Series B:** Real estate investments.
- **Series C:** E-commerce store.

If Series A is sued, Series B and C are protected.

**Available in:** Delaware, Nevada, Wyoming (with restrictions).

## Advanced asset protection strategies

### Strategy 1: Multiple LLCs

Instead of having one business in one LLC, you can have multiple LLCs for different assets or business lines.

**Example:**
- **Operating LLC:** Business operations -- Accounts receivable, contracts
- **Property LLC:** Intellectual property -- Trademarks, patents, copyrights
- **Real Estate LLC:** Real estate -- Properties
- **Holding LLC:** Investments -- Stocks, funds
**Benefit:** If someone sues the Operating LLC, they cannot reach the real estate or intellectual property.

### Strategy 2: LLC + Trust

Combining an LLC with a trust adds another layer of protection.

**Benefits:**
- The trust owns the LLC, not you personally.
- Trust assets are protected from your personal creditors.
- The trust can have inheritance and succession provisions.
- Greater privacy (the trust is not public like the LLC).

**Types of trusts for asset protection:**
- **Revocable Living Trust:** Avoid probate, privacy
- **Irrevocable Trust:** Maximum protection, but you lose control
- **Asset Protection Trust:** Designed specifically for protection
- **Family Trust:** Protection and inheritance planning

### Strategy 4: International Holding Company

For larger asset portfolios, a multi-jurisdictional structure:

- **Holding:** Panama, BVI -- Owner of subsidiaries
- **Operating:** U.S. (Wyoming/Florida) -- Business operations
- **IP Holding:** Delaware or Nevada -- Intellectual property

**Benefits:**
- Multiple layers of protection.

- Privacy.
- Succession planning.

**Warning:** This structure is complex and expensive. Only recommended for significant assets (>$500,000).

## What asset protection is NOT

It is important to distinguish between **legal** asset protection and **tax evasion** or **fraud**.

- **Separating personal from business assets:** Hiding assets from the IRS
- **Using legal structures to limit liability:** Not declaring income
- **Planning inheritance and succession:** Transferring assets to avoid paying existing debts
- **Choosing states with favorable laws:** Using structures for money laundering
**Asset protection does not exempt you from paying taxes.** You must declare your income in your country of residence and comply with all tax obligations.

## By country: specific considerations

### If you reside in Mexico

- The LLC is generally treated as a transparent entity (not a corporation).
- You can use the LLC to protect assets without double taxation.
- The Mexico-U.S. treaty facilitates structuring.
- Consider a Mexican trust (fideicomiso) combined with an LLC.

### If you reside in Colombia

- Colombia may treat the LLC as a corporation (hybrid mismatch).
- LLC asset protection is effective against third parties.
- You must declare LLC assets on your wealth statement.
- Consult with a Colombian accountant before structuring.

### If you reside in Argentina

- The Argentina-U.S. treaty allows efficient structures.
- The LLC is generally treated as a transparent entity.
- Argentine currency controls make U.S. bank accounts attractive.
- Consider an LLC + trust structure for maximum protection.

### If you reside in Peru

- Peru does not have a tax treaty with the U.S.
- The LLC may be treated as a corporation.
- Asset protection is still effective.
- Evaluate specific tax treatment with a Peruvian accountant.

### If you reside in Brazil

- Brazil does not have a tax treaty with the U.S. (suspended).
- Brazil may treat the LLC as a corporation.
- Asset protection can be complex.
- Consider alternative structures if the amount is significant.

## Practical cases

### Case 1: Consultant with basic protection

**Situation:** Pedro is a marketing consultant in Mexico. He bills $12,000/month to U.S. clients. He has an LLC in Wyoming.

**Structure:**
- LLC in Wyoming (disregarded entity).
- Mercury account.
- Personal Wise account.
- Complete Operating Agreement.
- Professional liability insurance.

**What it protects:**
- His personal savings in Mexico.
- His home.
- His personal investments.

**Risk covered:** If a client sues his LLC over a failed campaign, they can only claim the $50,000 in Mercury, not the $200,000 in his personal account in Mexico.

### Case 2: Agency owner with medium structure

**Situation:** Maria owns a digital marketing agency with 5 employees. She bills $50,000/month. She has two business lines: services and online courses.

**Structure:**
- **LLC 1 (Operating):** Agency operations (services).
- **LLC 2 (Courses):** Online courses and intellectual property.
- Both in Wyoming.
- Separate Mercury and Relay accounts.
- Revocable trust owning both LLCs.

**What it protects:**
- If a client sues over services, only LLC 1 is affected.
- Online courses (LLC 2) are protected.
- Trust assets are separate from her personal assets.

### Case 3: Business owner with advanced structure

**Situation:** Carlos has an e-commerce business billing $300,000/year. He has 3 partners. He owns 2 properties in the U.S.

**Structure:**
- **Holding Company** in Panama.
- **Operating LLC** in Wyoming (e-commerce).
- **Real Estate LLC** in Florida (properties).
- **Irrevocable trust** owning the Holding.
- Accounts at Mercury, Relay, and a local U.S. bank.

**What it protects:** All his business and personal assets are structured in layers that make it extremely difficult for a creditor to reach the assets.

## Common myths about asset protection

### Myth 1: "The LLC protects all my assets"

**Reality:** The LLC protects assets inside the LLC from your personal creditors, and your personal assets from the LLC's creditors. It does not protect against fraud, tax non-compliance, or piercing the corporate veil.

### Myth 2: "If I have an LLC, I don't need insurance"

**Reality:** The LLC and insurance are complementary. The LLC limits liability; insurance covers defense costs and potential damages.

### Myth 3: "Putting everything in the LLC is better"

**Reality:** Having all your assets in one LLC exposes them all if the LLC is sued. Better to have multiple LLCs or layers of protection.

### Myth 4: "Asset protection is only for the rich"

**Reality:** Anyone with a business should have basic protection. An LLC costs as little as $100-200 per year.

## Conclusion

Asset protection is not a luxury; it is a necessity for any international entrepreneur. A well-structured U.S. LLC is the first step, but depending on the size of your assets, you may need additional layers.

The key is to:
1. **Separate** your personal assets from business assets.
2. **Use the LLC correctly** (separate accounts, compliance up to date).
3. **Consider additional layers** (multiple LLCs, trusts) based on your risk level.
4. **Maintain compliance** so you do not lose protection.
5. **Consult with professionals** before structuring.
