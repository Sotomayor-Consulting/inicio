EIN stands for **Employer Identification Number**. It is a tax identification number issued by the IRS (Internal Revenue Service), the federal tax authority of the United States.

The EIN functions as the company's tax identification number. It is somewhat similar to a Tax ID or CNPJ in other countries.

## Who issues the EIN?

The EIN is issued by the IRS. The IRS uses this number to identify businesses and commercial structures for tax and administrative purposes.

## What is an EIN for?

The EIN is essential for operating a business in the United States. Here are its most common uses:

### Open business bank accounts

Many banks and fintechs require the EIN to open corporate accounts.

### File tax returns

The EIN is used for IRS forms, informational returns, tax reports, and compliance filings.

### Hire employees

If the company will have employees in the U.S., it will typically need an EIN.

### Access financial platforms

Many international platforms require an EIN for payment processors, fintech platforms, merchant accounts, Stripe, and similar tools.

### Separate the business from the person

The EIN helps the company have its own tax identity before the IRS and financial institutions.

## Is an EIN the same as an SSN?

**No.** This is a very common confusion.

| Number | For whom | Use |
|--------|----------|-----|
| **SSN** (Social Security Number) | Individuals | Personal identification |
| **EIN** (Employer Identification Number) | Businesses | Business tax identification |

A company can have an EIN even if the owner does not have an SSN.

## Can a foreigner obtain an EIN?

**Yes.** A non-resident alien can obtain an EIN for their LLC or company in the U.S.

It is not mandatory to have U.S. residency, a Green Card, or a Social Security Number. However, the process must be done correctly before the IRS.

## When is the EIN requested?

Usually after incorporating the LLC or company. The process typically includes:

1. Company registration.
2. Application to the IRS.
3. Validation of corporate information.

## What information is needed to apply for an EIN?

Typically required:

- Legal name of the company.
- State of incorporation.
- Company address.
- Owner information.
- Type of business activity.

Depending on the case, the IRS may request additional information.

## Does having an EIN mean I am already paying taxes?

**Not necessarily.** Having an EIN does not automatically mean the company owes taxes, generates taxes, has business activity, or is invoicing.

The EIN simply identifies the company for tax purposes before the IRS.

Tax obligations will depend on the activity, structure, income, owner's tax residency, and actual business operations.

## How long does it take to get an EIN?

The time may vary depending on the application method and the IRS workload. In some cases it can be quick, and in others it may take longer. There are also differences depending on whether the owner has an SSN, whether the applicant is a foreigner, and the method used.

## What happens if my LLC does not have an EIN?

Without an EIN, the company will often have limitations in:

- Opening bank accounts.
- Operating financially.
- Filing forms.
- Accessing payment platforms.
- Formalizing commercial operations.

That is why the EIN is usually one of the most important steps after incorporating the LLC.

## Common mistake: believing the EIN "authorizes" you to work in the U.S.

The EIN does **NOT** grant a visa, give immigration permission, authorize personal employment in the U.S., or generate residency. It is simply a business tax identification number.

## Does the EIN expire?

No. The EIN typically does not expire. However, the company must remain in compliance with the IRS, the state, annual reports, and required filings.

## Conclusion

The EIN is one of the fundamental elements for operating a business in the United States. It allows the company to be identified for tax purposes and is usually necessary for banks, financial platforms, IRS filings, and international commercial operations.

But obtaining the EIN is only part of the process. The most important thing is to structure the company correctly from the start and understand the obligations the LLC will have.
