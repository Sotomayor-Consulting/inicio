**Tax protection** is a set of legal strategies that allow entrepreneurs to reduce their tax burden, protect their assets, and keep their businesses in compliance with the law. Unlike tax evasion (illegal), tax protection uses the very instruments that the law provides to pay only the tax due -- no more, no less.

In this complete guide, we explain the main tax protection strategies for entrepreneurs, from basic tax planning to advanced international structures.

## 1. What is Tax Protection?

**Tax protection** (or tax planning) is the preventive activity of organizing business and assets in a legitimate and legal way to reduce the tax burden.

### Fundamental Concepts

- **Tax evasion:** Illegal action to avoid paying taxes (fraud, concealment)
- **Tax avoidance:** Use of legal loopholes to reduce taxes (gray area)
- **Tax planning:** Advance and legal organization of operations to optimize tax burden
> **Golden rule:** Tax protection must be **preventive**, not corrective. Planning before acting is legal; trying to fix afterwards can be considered fraud.

### Why is it important?

- **Legal reduction of the tax burden** -- savings of 30% to 60% in some cases
- **Asset protection** -- shielding against tax executions
- **Legal certainty** -- peace of mind to do business without tax risks
- **Competitiveness** -- more resources to reinvest in the business

## 2. Basic Tax Protection Strategies

### 2.1 Choosing the Right Tax Regime

Choosing the right tax regime is the most important decision for any company's tax protection.

- **Simplified Regime:** Varies by country -- 4% to 15% -- Micro and small businesses
- **Presumed Profit:** Varies by country -- 11% to 14% -- Service and trade companies
- **Actual Profit:** No limit -- 15% to 34% -- Companies with variable margins or high expenses
### 2.2 Strategic Profit Distribution

In many countries, profits distributed to shareholders have significant tax benefits.

**Strategy:** Instead of paying a high salary (taxed by personal income tax), distribute profits periodically when it is more tax-efficient.

### 2.3 Allowed Tax Deductions

Maximizing tax deductions is one of the simplest forms of tax protection:

**Common deductible expenses:**
- Salaries and payroll taxes
- Commercial property rent
- Office supplies and inputs
- Third-party services (accounting, legal, consulting)
- Asset depreciation
- Vehicle expenses used in the activity
- Business financing interest
- Employee health insurance

**NON-deductible expenses:**
- Fines for tax infractions
- Personal expenses of shareholders (unrelated to the activity)
- Non-accrued provisions

### 2.4 Retirement Planning

Entrepreneurs can choose between different retirement regimes:

- **Social Security:** Percentage on salary -- Public pension
- **Individual Retirement Account:** Tax-deductible (with limits) -- Retirement supplement
- **Company Pension Plan:** Deductible for the company -- Benefits for employees and shareholders
## 3. Advanced Tax Protection Structures

### 3.1 Asset Holding Company

The asset holding company is one of the most efficient structures for entrepreneurs' tax protection:

**How it works:**
- Real estate and personal assets are transferred to a holding company
- The holding company leases the assets to the operating company
- The rent paid is deductible for the operating company and taxed favorably in the holding

**Tax advantages:**
- Rent paid to the holding reduces the taxable profit of the operating company
- The holding taxes rent at reduced rates
- The entrepreneur can distribute profits from the holding tax-free

### 3.2 Offshore Company

An offshore company can be used as part of an international tax protection strategy:

**Typical structure:**

**Tax advantages:**
- Tax deferral: taxes are paid only when profits are repatriated
- Access to international treaties to reduce withholding tax
- Ability to structure international investments without creating tax presence

> **Important:** Many countries have tightened offshore legislation (CRS, FATCA, transparency laws). Specialized advisory is essential to remain compliant.

### 3.3 Estate Planning with Tax Protection

Estate planning allows transferring assets to heirs with reduced tax burden:

**Instruments:**
- **Family holding:** Transfers business interests with protection clauses
- **Usufruct:** Parents retain the right to profits while alive
- **Gifts with clauses:** Transfers assets during lifetime with usufruct reservation
- **Life insurance:** Benefit paid to heirs without going through probate

- **Startup regime:** Simplified regimes for emerging companies

### 4.2 Export

- **Drawback:** Suspension of taxes on imported inputs for export
- **Digital export:** Digital service companies may have tax benefits

### 4.3 Real Estate Sector

- **REITs:** Tax-advantaged structures for real estate investment
- **Real estate development:** Special taxation regimes
- **Leasing:** Tax benefits for rental income

## 5. Risks and Common Mistakes

### 5.1 What to AVOID

- **Distributing profits without regular accounting:** Deduction disallowed by tax authority
- **Mixing personal and business expenses:** Deduction disallowed and fines
- **Using offshore companies without reporting:** Significant penalties and possible criminal prosecution
- **Not doing annual tax planning:** Lost tax savings opportunities
### 5.2 Legislative Change Risks

- Ongoing tax reforms in various countries
- Increased international tax transparency (CRS, FATCA, BEPS)
- Stricter rules for tax planning (GAAR, anti-avoidance rules)

> **Tip:** Tax protection should be reviewed annually with specialized advisors to adapt to legislative changes.

## 6. International Tax Protection

### 6.1 Tax Residence vs. Source

- **Residence:** Country taxes worldwide income of resident -- Choose strategic tax residence
- **Source:** Country taxes income generated in its territory -- Structure to reduce source taxation
### 6.2 International Tax Treaties

Tax treaties to avoid double taxation allow:

- Reducing withholding tax on dividends, interest, and royalties
- Defining which country has the right to tax
- Resolving tax residence conflicts
