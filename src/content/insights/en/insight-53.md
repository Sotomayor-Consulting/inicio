**Stripe** is a payment platform that takes compliance and risk management very seriously. Unlike traditional banks, Stripe can unilaterally suspend or close accounts if it detects activities that violate its Terms of Service or are considered high-risk.

Thousands of entrepreneurs have their Stripe accounts closed every year — often without prior notice and with funds held for up to 180 days. In this guide, we explain **how to prevent Stripe from closing your account** and what to do if it happens.

## 1. Why Does Stripe Close Accounts?

### Main Reasons

| Reason | Description | Severity |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| **Terms of Service violation** | Prohibited business or disallowed activity | ⚠️ Critical |
| **High chargeback risk** | Chargeback rate above 1% | ⚠️ High |
| **Fraud or fraud suspicion** | Suspicious transactions or inconsistencies | ⚠️ Critical |
| **Incomplete KYC** | Expired documents or inconsistent information | ⚠️ Medium |
| **Risk profile change** | Business changed activities without notice | ⚠️ High |
| **High cancellation rate** | Too many refunds or cancellations | ⚠️ Medium |
| **Suspicious activity** | Unusual transaction patterns | ⚠️ High |
| **BOI/AML non-compliance** | Regulatory obligations not met | ⚠️ Critical |

> **Statistic:** According to community reports, approximately 2% to 5% of Stripe accounts are closed or suspended at some point. Most closures occur within the first 6 months of operation.

## 2. Golden Rules to Keep Your Account Active

### Rule 1: Know the Terms of Service

Read and understand Stripe's Terms of Service. They change periodically, and it's your responsibility to stay updated.

**Critical points:**
- Prohibited businesses (see full list in the next section)
- Country restrictions
- Verification obligations
- Appeal deadlines

### Rule 2: Keep Your Documentation Updated

| Document | Update Frequency |
|----------|-----------------|
| **Identity document** | Renew before expiration |
| **Proof of address** | Less than 3 months old (when requested) |
| **Company documents** | Whenever there is a change |
| **BOI** | 30 days after any change |
| **Bank account** | Notify Stripe if it changes |

### Rule 3: Monitor Your Chargeback Rate

The ideal chargeback rate is **below 0.5%**. Above 1%, Stripe may put your account under review. Above 2%, closure is almost certain.

**How to reduce chargebacks:**
- Use clear billing descriptors (the name that appears on the customer's statement)
- Send confirmation and tracking emails
- Offer responsive customer support
- Implement 3D Secure (Stripe Radar)
- Clear and visible refund policy

### Rule 4: Maintain Consistent Sales Volume

Stripe monitors transaction patterns. Sudden spikes without history can trigger risk alerts.

**Best practices:**
- Increase volume gradually
- Avoid high-value transactions early on
- Keep an average ticket size consistent with your industry

### Rule 5: Have a Professional Website or App

Stripe analyzes your website or app during verification and continuously after approval.

**Your page must have:**
- Privacy policy
- Terms of service
- Refund and return policy
- Contact information (email, phone, address)
- Clear description of products or services

## 3. Prohibited and High-Risk Businesses

### Prohibited (Immediate Closure)

- Cryptocurrencies (except licensed exchanges)
- Gambling and betting
- Adult content
- Weapons and ammunition
- Drugs and controlled substances
- Pyramid schemes
- Sale of false documents

### High Risk (Constant Review)

| Category | Risk | Strategy |
|----------|------|----------|
| **Health and wellness** | Supplements, medical equipment | Additional documentation, certifications |
| **Travel** | Agencies, bookings | Chargeback anticipation, reserve fund |
| **Financial services** | Consulting, currency exchange | Licenses and verifications |
| **Subscriptions** | Recurring models | Clear cancellation policy |
| **Digital products** | Courses, e-books | Immediate delivery and support |

## 4. Warning Signs Stripe Monitors

### Customer Behavior

- Many declined payment attempts
- Multiple chargebacks from the same customer
- Transactions from high-risk countries

### Seller Behavior

- **Withdrawal speed:** Withdrawing funds immediately after receiving can look suspicious
- **Sudden business model change:** Altering products or services without notice
- **Very high-value transactions:** Especially on new accounts
- **Many refunds:** Refund rate above 10% triggers alerts

### Technical

- **Frequent bank account changes**
- **IP from unsupported countries** accessing the dashboard
- **Unsecured integration** (no SSL, exposed data)
- **Excessive testing** in production mode

## 5. How Stripe Notifies Problems

### Alert Levels

| Level | Notification | Timeline | Action Required |
|-------|-------------|----------|-----------------|
| **1 - Warning** | Email with suggestions | N/A | Adjust practices |
| **2 - Document request** | Email + dashboard | 7-14 days | Send requested documents |
| **3 - Temporary suspension** | Email + dashboard | Immediate | Resolve cause + contact support |
| **4 - Closure** | Formal email | Immediate | Request fund release |

### What NOT to Do When Receiving an Alert

- ❌ Ignore the email or notification
- ❌ Create a new account (may be considered fraud)
- ❌ Transfer operations to another account without resolving the issue
- ❌ Change company data without justification

## 6. Action Plan: Steps to Avoid Closure

### Step 1: Perform an Internal Audit

Review your Stripe account regularly:

- [ ] Are documents up to date?
- [ ] Is the chargeback rate below 0.5%?
- [ ] Are products/services within Stripe's rules?
- [ ] Is the website complete and professional?
- [ ] Has BOI been filed (if applicable)?

### Step 2: Diversify Your Payment Processors

Never rely exclusively on Stripe. Have at least one alternative processor configured.

**Recommended alternatives:**
- **Paypal** — international payments
- **Mercado Pago** — Latin America
- **Payoneer** — payment accounts
- **Authorize.net** — traditional processing
- **Square** — in-person and online businesses
- **Adyen** — high-volume businesses

### Step 3: Maintain a Reserve Fund

Stripe can hold funds for up to 180 days when closing an account. Have a financial reserve equivalent to at least 3 months of revenue processed by Stripe.

### Step 4: Communicate Proactively with Stripe

If you plan to:
- Change your business model
- Launch a high-risk product
- Significantly increase volume

**Contact Stripe beforehand** to check if additional approval is needed.

### Step 5: Use Stripe's Compliance Tools

- **Stripe Radar** — fraud prevention system
- **3D Secure** — additional buyer authentication
- **Stripe Identity** — customer identity verification
- **Webhooks** — real-time monitoring

## 7. What to Do If Your Account Is Closed

### Step 1: Don't Panic

Closure doesn't necessarily mean you did something wrong. It could be a false alarm or an automated decision.

### Step 2: Read Stripe's Communication

Check:
- The reason for the closure
- The requested documents (if any)
- The deadline to appeal

### Step 3: Gather Documents

Depending on the case, you may need:

- **Proof of identity** (passport, ID)
- **Proof of address**
- **Proof of product delivery** (for e-commerce)
- **Invoices and receipts**
- **Licenses and certifications** (if applicable)
- **Company policies** (refund, privacy)

### Step 4: Contact Stripe Support

- Open a ticket from the dashboard
- Be objective and polite
- Explain your business model clearly
- Attach all requested documentation

> **Tip:** Stripe responds faster to well-documented and polite tickets. Avoid aggressive language or legal threats — this can delay the process.

### Step 5: Prepare to Migrate

While waiting for resolution:

- Set up an alternative processor
- Notify your customers about possible changes
- Keep detailed records of pending transactions

## 9. Stripe Radar: Fraud Prevention

**Stripe Radar** is a powerful tool that helps prevent fraudulent transactions and reduce chargebacks

### Recommended Settings

| Rule | Action | Description |
|------|--------|-------------|
| **Block high-risk country IPs** | Block | Prevents transactions from high-fraud countries |
| **Block temporary emails** | Block | Prevents disposable email use |
| **Require 3D Secure above $100** | Require authentication | Reduces chargebacks on high amounts |
| **Block international debit cards** | Block | Prevents international card fraud |
| **Manually review suspicious transactions** | Review | Allows analysis before approval |

- **Radar for Fraud Teams:** $0.02/transaction + $0.10/manually reviewed transaction
- **Radar (free):** Basic rules included in all accounts

## 10. Real Stories and Lessons Learned

### Case 1: The Supplement E-commerce Store

**Problem:** Supplement store had its account closed for "unapproved health products."

**Solution:** The business owner obtained health certifications, submitted documentation, and appealed. The account was reactivated in 30 days.

**Lesson:** Health businesses need additional documentation from the start.

### Case 2: The Subscription Startup

**Problem:** 3% chargeback rate due to customers unhappy with cancellation difficulty.

**Solution:** They implemented 1-click cancellation, a clear refund policy, and 3D Secure. The rate dropped to 0.3%.

**Lesson:** Chargebacks are avoidable with good customer service practices.

### Case 3: The Digital Marketing Freelancer

**Problem:** Account closed for accessing the dashboard from an unsupported country (Venezuela).

**Solution:** Explained he was traveling, presented a passport with stamps and proof of address in the original country. Account reactivated.

**Lesson:** Use a VPN or notify Stripe if you need to access from unsupported countries.

## Conclusion

Preventing **Stripe from closing your account** is primarily a matter of compliance, transparency, and good operational practices. Most closures can be prevented with proper planning.

The keys to keeping your account active are:
1. Know and follow the Terms of Service
2. Keep documentation always updated
3. Monitor chargebacks and refunds
4. Have a professional and complete website
5. Diversify payment processors
6. Communicate changes to Stripe proactively
