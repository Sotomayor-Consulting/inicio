If you have bank accounts, investments, or financial assets outside the United States, you have likely heard about **FATCA**. But **what exactly is FATCA and how does it affect you?** The Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act (FATCA) is a U.S. law designed to combat tax evasion through offshore accounts.

Below, we explain everything you need to know about FATCA and your obligations as a taxpayer.

## 1. What Is FATCA?

FATCA (Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act) was enacted in 2010 as part of the Hiring Incentives to Restore Employment (HIRE) Act. Its primary goal is to **detect and prevent tax evasion** by identifying U.S. taxpayers who hold financial accounts abroad.

### How does it work?
FATCA requires **foreign financial institutions (FFIs)** —banks, investment funds, insurance companies, etc.— to report to the IRS information about accounts held by U.S. citizens or residents.

If a foreign financial institution fails to comply with these reporting requirements, a **30% withholding tax** is applied to certain U.S.-source payments (such as interest, dividends, and capital gains).

## 2. Who Does FATCA Affect?

FATCA affects:

### Affected persons:
- **U.S. citizens** (living inside or outside the U.S.)
- **Lawful permanent residents** (Green Card holders)
- **Tax residents** under the substantial presence test
- **U.S. entities** with foreign accounts

### Exceptions:
- Citizens who renounced their citizenship (though special rules apply for "expatriates" with high income or assets)
- Nonresidents with no tax ties to the U.S.

## 3. Form 8938: Statement of Specified Foreign Financial Assets

The main FATCA reporting tool for individuals is **Form 8938**, which must be filed with your annual tax return (Form 1040).

### When must you file it?

| Filing Status | Asset Value if Living in the U.S. | Asset Value if Living Abroad |
|---|---|---|
| Single or separate filer | More than $50,000 (last day) or $75,000 (any time) | More than $200,000 (last day) or $300,000 (any time) |
| Married (joint filing) | More than $100,000 (last day) or $150,000 (any time) | More than $400,000 (last day) or $600,000 (any time) |

### Assets you must report:
- Foreign bank and investment accounts
- Stocks, bonds, and securities issued by non-U.S. entities
- Interests in foreign investment funds
- Life insurance or annuity contracts with cash value issued by foreign entities
- Certain interests in foreign entities (such as partnerships, trusts, or estates)

## 4. FATCA vs. FBAR: What Is the Difference?

One of the most common confusions is the difference between **FATCA** and **FBAR**. Although both require reporting foreign accounts, they are distinct requirements:

| Aspect | FATCA (Form 8938) | FBAR (FinCEN Form 114) |
|---|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Report foreign financial assets | Report foreign bank and financial accounts |
| **Form** | Form 8938 (with your tax return) | FinCEN Form 114 (separate filing) |
| **Threshold** | Varies by residency and filing status ($50,000 – $600,000) | $10,000 (aggregate value at any time during the year) |
| **Covered assets** | Accounts, stocks, bonds, funds, insurance, entity interests | Bank accounts, investment accounts, insurance accounts |
| **Filing** | With your Form 1040 (annual) | Separately, by October 15 (electronic filing) |
| **Penalty for non-filing** | $10,000 per violation (up to $50,000 for continued violation) | Up to $10,000 for non-willful; up to $100,000 or 50% of balance for willful |

> **Important:** You may be required to file **both** forms if you meet each one's thresholds.

## 5. How Does FATCA Affect Foreign Financial Institutions?

Foreign financial institutions (FFIs) must:

1. **Register** with the IRS and obtain a GIIN (Global Intermediary Identification Number)
2. **Identify** their U.S. customers through due diligence procedures
3. **Report** annually to the IRS information about U.S. taxpayer accounts
4. **Withhold 30%** on certain U.S.-source payments if non-compliant

### Consequences for non-compliant FFIs:
- 30% withholding on U.S.-source payments
- Possible exclusion from the U.S. financial system
- Reputational damage

## 6. Intergovernmental Agreements (IGAs)

To facilitate FATCA implementation, the U.S. has signed **Intergovernmental Agreements (IGAs)** with over 100 countries. There are two models:

### Model 1 (IGA 1):
- FFIs report information to their local tax authority
- The local authority automatically exchanges information with the IRS
- Examples: Mexico, Colombia, Peru, Chile, Argentina, Spain

### Model 2 (IGA 2):
- FFIs report directly to the IRS
- The local government agrees to assist in information collection if needed
- Examples: Switzerland, Japan

### Countries without an IGA:
- FFIs in these countries must sign a direct agreement with the IRS or face 30% withholding

## 7. Penalties for FATCA Non-Compliance

Failing to file Form 8938 when required can result in:

- **$10,000 penalty** for failure to file
- **Additional $10,000 penalty** for each month of delay after receiving an IRS notice (up to $50,000 for continued violation)
- **Extended statute of limitations**: The IRS has 6 years to audit your return if you omit more than $5,000 in reportable foreign assets (instead of the usual 3 years)
- **Potential criminal charges** in cases of deliberate tax evasion

### Voluntary Disclosure Programs:
If you have not complied with FATCA, the IRS offers programs such as the **Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures** and the **Offshore Voluntary Disclosure Program (OVDP)** to regularize your situation with reduced penalties.

## 8. FATCA and Foreign LLC Owners

If you are a U.S. citizen or resident and own a **foreign LLC** (including an LLC in your home country):

- You must report your ownership on Form 8938 if you exceed the thresholds
- You may also need to file **Form 5471** (Information Return of U.S. Persons With Respect to Certain Foreign Corporations) or **Form 8865** (for foreign partnerships)
- The foreign LLC may qualify as a "foreign entity" requiring additional reporting

## 9. FATCA and Expatriates (Citizenship Renunciation)

If you renounced your U.S. citizenship or green card, FATCA may still affect you:

- If you are a "covered expatriate" (annual income > $200,000 or net worth > $2 million), you must file **Form 8854**
- Foreign financial institutions may still report your accounts if you maintain U.S. ties
- You may be subject to **Section 877A** (exit tax)

## 10. Recommendations for FATCA Compliance

### Practical steps:
1. **Identify your foreign assets**: Take inventory of all accounts and investments outside the U.S.
2. **Determine if you exceed the thresholds**: Check the amounts based on your filing status and residence
3. **File Form 8938 on time**: Include it with your annual tax return
4. **Don't forget FBAR**: Both forms may be required simultaneously
5. **Consult a specialist**: FATCA rules are complex and penalties for non-compliance are severe

## Conclusion

**FATCA** is a fundamental law in the fight against international tax evasion. Understanding **what FATCA is** and how it affects you is essential if you are a U.S. citizen or resident with foreign financial assets. From Form 8938 to intergovernmental agreements and non-compliance penalties, every aspect of this law has important implications for your tax return.
