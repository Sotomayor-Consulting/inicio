Mercury and Relay have become two of the most popular banking options for Latin American entrepreneurs who form an LLC in the United States. Their 100% online process, remote account opening, and integration with platforms like Stripe make them ideal for international digital businesses.

However, many people have experienced the dreaded **account closure or freeze** without warning. In most cases, these problems are due to non-compliance with banking compliance policies, not bank errors.

In this article, we explain the most common causes of problems with Mercury and Relay, and how to avoid them.

## Why can Mercury or Relay close your account?

These banks operate under U.S. federal and state regulations and are subject to strict rules regarding:

- **KYC (Know Your Customer):** They must verify the identity of all account owners.
- **AML (Anti-Money Laundering):** They must detect and report suspicious activity.
- **OFAC (Office of Foreign Assets Control):** They must ensure they do not do business with sanctioned persons or countries.
- **BSA (Bank Secrecy Act):** They must report transactions exceeding certain thresholds.

If the bank detects something it cannot properly verify, **it will prefer to close the account rather than risk a regulatory fine**.

## Common causes of problems

### 1. Incomplete or inconsistent documentation

When opening the account, you provided information about your LLC and its owners. If the bank requests additional documentation and you do not respond in time, or the documentation does not match what was registered, they may close the account.

**Examples:**

- The LLC name on the bank does not exactly match the Certificate of Formation.
- The personal address does not match the passport.
- You do not respond to additional identity verification requests.

### 2. Banking activity that appears suspicious

Bank algorithms monitor transaction patterns. Certain behaviors can trigger alerts:

- **Frequent cash deposits:** Possible money laundering
- **Transfers from personal accounts without explanation:** Mixing personal and business funds
- **Multiple small transfers:** Possible structuring (smurfing)
- **Transactions to high-risk countries:** Possible sanctions violation
- **Prolonged inactivity followed by sudden activity:** Possible account misuse
### 3. Not keeping the LLC in compliance

Both Mercury and Relay may request proof that your LLC is in good standing. If your LLC is suspended for not filing the Annual Report, the bank may freeze or close your account.

**This includes:**

- Annual Report not filed.
- Registered Agent expired.
- LLC dissolved by the state.
- No valid EIN.

### 4. Mixing personal and business funds

Your LLC account is **exclusively for business use**. Depositing personal income, paying personal expenses, or receiving personal transfers can be considered a violation of the terms of service.

### 5. Not responding to information requests

Banks perform periodic compliance reviews. If you receive an email requesting additional information and do not respond within the given timeframe (usually 30–60 days), your account may be automatically closed.

> **Statistic:** Over 60% of account closures at Mercury and Relay for international LLCs occur because the owner did not respond to documentation requests in time.

## How to avoid problems: best practices

### When opening the account

- **Use the exact same LLC name** as it appears on the Certificate of Formation.
- **Ensure your passport is valid** and the information matches exactly.
- **Have the Operating Agreement ready** in case it is requested.
- **Use a real, verifiable personal address** in your country of residence.
- **Do not use virtual addresses** as your personal address.

### During operation

- **Keep income and expenses clearly separated:** Only use the account for business transactions.
- **Document the source of funds:** If you receive large transfers, keep the contracts or invoices that support them.
- **Avoid cash deposits:** Most of these banks do not accept cash and it can trigger alerts.
- **Do not receive transfers from unrelated third parties:** If someone who is not a client transfers you money, have documentation justifying it.
- **Respond immediately** to any information request from the bank.

### LLC maintenance

- **File the Annual Report** on time every year.
- **Keep the Registered Agent active** and paid.
- **File informational forms** with the IRS (Form 5472, 1120 pro forma, etc.).
- **Do not let the LLC be suspended** for any reason.
- **Maintain a valid registered address** in the state of incorporation.

## What to do if your account is already frozen or closed

### Step 1: Determine the cause

Check the bank's email to understand why it was closed or frozen. The most common reasons are:

- Documentation request not answered.
- Suspicious activity detected.
- LLC not in good standing.

### Step 2: Respond immediately

If the bank requests documentation and you are still within the timeframe:

- Gather all requested documents (Certificate of Formation, EIN Letter, Operating Agreement, passport, proof of address).
- Respond through the official channel indicated in the email.
- Be clear and complete in your response.

### Step 3: Regularize the LLC

If the LLC was suspended:

- Pay outstanding penalties.
- File missing Annual Reports.
- Request reinstatement with the Secretary of State.

### Step 4: Look for banking alternatives

If the account was permanently closed, consider these alternatives:

- **Wise Business:** International payments, multiple currencies -- Yes
- **Novo:** Small businesses, accounting integration -- Yes
- **Stripe Treasury:** Businesses already using Stripe -- Yes
- **Payoneer:** Freelancers and marketplaces -- Yes
- **Traditional bank account:** Businesses with physical operations -- Depends on bank

## Common mistakes leading to closure

- **Not reading bank emails:** Many entrepreneurs ignore compliance emails because they are in English or look like spam.
- **Using the account for personal expenses:** Personal Amazon purchases, personal subscriptions, etc.
- **Transferring large amounts without justification:** Without a contract or invoice supporting the source.
- **Not maintaining minimum balance:** Some accounts require a minimum balance or regular activity.
- **Changing country of residence without notifying:** If you move, you must update your information.

## Checklist: How to keep your account safe

- [ ] Respond to bank emails within 7 days.
- [ ] Keep LLC in good standing (Annual Report up to date).
- [ ] Use the account only for business expenses.
- [ ] Save invoices and contracts for large income.
- [ ] Do not deposit cash.
- [ ] Keep Registered Agent active.
- [ ] Update information if you change address or phone.
- [ ] Have digital copies of Certificate of Formation, EIN, and Operating Agreement.
- [ ] Check the email associated with the bank at least once a week.

## Conclusion

Mercury and Relay are powerful financial tools for Latin American entrepreneurs with LLCs in the U.S., but they are not infallible. The key to avoiding problems is simple: **comply with the bank's policies and keep your LLC in good standing**.

Most account closures are due to failure to respond to documentation requests or because the LLC was suspended. Both problems are preventable with basic compliance management.
